# Part One · Section 1 — Management

NVIDIA is a founder-led company to an unusual degree. **Jensen Huang** co-founded NVIDIA in 1993 with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem and has been CEO ever since — a 33-year tenure that spans the near-death of the company in the late 1990s, the invention of the GPU category (GeForce 256, 1999), the CUDA bet in 2006, and the AI era. Malachowsky remains a senior technology executive; Priem left long ago.

The management fact that matters most is the CUDA decision. In 2006 — six years before deep learning took off — Huang made every NVIDIA graphics card programmable for general-purpose computing and gave the software away, subsidizing an ecosystem with no near-term payoff. When AlexNet won ImageNet in 2012 training on two NVIDIA gaming GPUs, the world's AI researchers were already sitting on NVIDIA hardware and NVIDIA libraries. Today's moat was a management decision made two decades early, at real cost, against short-term logic. That is the pattern to watch: this team repeatedly spends early on platform shifts (ray tracing, data-center networking via the 2020 Mellanox acquisition, rack-scale systems, and now "AI factories") before the revenue is visible.

Around Huang: **Colette Kress** has been CFO since 2013 and is the voice of the company's financial discipline — conservative guidance, buybacks scaled to cash flow, and (until recently) a nearly debt-free balance sheet. The operating model is famously flat: Huang has dozens of direct reports, no standing 1:1s, and strategy is argued in open meetings. Whatever one thinks of the style, the execution record is the data point: the company has hit an annual product cadence (Hopper → Blackwell → Blackwell Ultra → Vera Rubin) while growing revenue from $27.0B (fiscal 2023) to $215.9B (fiscal 2026) — an 8x increase in three years with gross margins intact.

Two management questions belong in any honest summary. First, **key-person concentration**: NVIDIA's strategy, supplier relationships, and customer trust run through one 63-year-old founder more than at any comparable company. Second, **capital allocation is changing character**: in 2026 the company issued $25B of debt, invested $30B in OpenAI's February 2026 funding round, and guaranteed up to $105B of a customer's data-center leases. The Kress-era "fortress balance sheet" story is becoming a "NVIDIA finances its own ecosystem" story. Part Two examines whether that is vision or the beginning of vendor financing.
