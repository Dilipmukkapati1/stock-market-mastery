Read this table as a phase change, not a growth streak. Fiscal 2023 was a *bad* year — crypto and pandemic over-ordering left NVIDIA taking a $1.22B inventory reserve and earning $4.4B. Four years later the same company earns $120.1B. Nothing in large-cap history compounds like this, which is precisely why the valuation chapter (Deep Research 4) is a debate about duration, not about the present.

## Segment economics

Data Center compute ($162.4B in fiscal 2026) carries the company; networking ($31.4B, +142%) is the fastest-growing piece and the strategically decisive one — whoever sells the fabric sells the architecture. Gross margin tells the rack story: as the product shifted from Hopper cards (HGX) to full Blackwell racks, NVIDIA took on more system content (memory, boards, assembly via partners) and margin diluted from 75.0% to 71.1%, then recovered to 75.0% by Q2 fiscal 2027 as Blackwell Ultra mix and pricing held. Management has repeatedly treated ~75% as the ceiling it defends rather than a floor it raises — pricing power is being spent on share and ecosystem, not on margin extraction. That is a choice worth noticing.

Operating leverage is extreme: Q2 fiscal 2027 operating expenses were $8.4B against $63.7B of operating income. R&D is the deliberate reinvestment — the annual cadence (Rubin, Rubin Ultra, Feynman in parallel development) is what that opex buys.

## The 2026 balance-sheet turn — the most important new fact in this book

For a decade NVIDIA's finances were boring: no debt drama, cash piling up, buybacks. In 2026 that changed, in one direction — NVIDIA began financing its own demand chain (all from filings and reported analysis of the Q2 fiscal 2027 10-Q):

- **June 2026:** issued $25.0B of senior notes — its largest debt raise, taking debt-to-equity from ~0.05x to ~0.15x. Still trivial leverage; the *decision* is the news.
- **February 2026:** invested $30B in OpenAI's funding round.
- **August 2026:** agreed residual-value guarantees behind OpenAI's leases at a planned Ohio data-center campus, initial payment obligation capped at **$105B**.
- **Q2 fiscal 2027 cash flow:** operating cash flow fell to $24.1B from $50.3B in Q1, while the company bought $15.8B of equity securities and returned $26.0B to shareholders. Filings also show ~$26B of commitments to rent back GPU capacity from cloud partners.
- **Supply commitments:** ~$95.2B of manufacturing and supply obligations, substantially due through fiscal 2027 (10-K) — the flip side of guaranteeing TSMC and memory suppliers their volumes.

None of this is hidden; all of it is disclosed. The analytical question is what it *is*. The bullish reading: NVIDIA is converting a fortress balance sheet into strategic lock-in — guaranteeing a customer's leases and renting back capacity keeps the ecosystem building on NVIDIA through any funding winter, and the amounts, while enormous in absolute terms, are sized against ~$100B of annual free cash flow. The bearish reading: this is how vendor-financed booms look from inside — revenue increasingly depends on counterparties whose credit NVIDIA itself backstops, so a demand air-pocket would hit the income statement and the balance sheet simultaneously. The guarantee cap ($105B) is roughly one year of free cash flow; that is the number to hold in mind.

Capital return continues regardless: ~$40.4B of buybacks in fiscal 2026, ~$26.0B returned in Q2 fiscal 2027 alone, and a September 2026 board addition of $150B bringing total remaining authorization to ~$235B through fiscal 2028.

## China and the H20 precedent

Fiscal 2026 gross margin carries a scar: a $4.5B charge in Q1 fiscal 2026 when the U.S. required licenses for the China-market H20, stranding inventory. Licenses for certain H20 shipments were issued in August 2025, and in January 2026 China approved H200 sales — initial shipments reported at 40,000–80,000 units around $32,000 each, with a reported 25% of the China revenue from these sales payable to the U.S. government under the arrangement. China is upside optionality at regulated prices, not a plannable business — and the $4.5B charge is the template for how export policy reaches the P&L without warning.
