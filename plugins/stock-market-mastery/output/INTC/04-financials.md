# Part One — The Product

Intel's portfolio is narrower than the conglomerate it replaced — deliberately. What remains is CPUs for clients and servers, the process technology underneath them, advanced packaging, and two partnership programs that exist so far as agreements. Each product below carries its honest status, because at Intel the distance between *announced* and *shipping* is where a decade of credibility was lost.

**Client: Panther Lake and Nova Lake.** Panther Lake (Core Ultra Series 3) launched at CES in January 2026 — the first high-volume product on the 18A process, made at Fab 52 in Arizona — and ramped through 2026, with management reporting 18A costs down ~40% since Q1. Its successor, **Nova Lake**, is the first 18A-P product, reported ahead of schedule, with management expecting roughly twice Panther Lake's design wins. Wildcat Lake (the value line) was described as sold out. Status: *shipping and ramping* — the portfolio's most real product line, selling into a PC market guided down through H1 2027.

**Servers: the Xeon line.** Xeon 6 (Granite Rapids) is the product of Intel's year: demand described as supply-tight through 2026, driving DCAI's +78% quarter. Behind it: **Clearwater Forest**, the first 18A server part, and **Diamond Rapids** on 18A-P — with Oracle planning OCI X14 systems around it — the generation that must hold the line against AMD's next EPYC and the Arm hosts. Status: *Granite Rapids shipping; successors announced and sampling.*

**AI accelerators: Gaudi's end, Crescent Island's beginning.** The Gaudi line is effectively a niche product now — an estimated ~1% share of AI accelerators, with AI *training* conceded to NVIDIA's ecosystem. The successor bet, named **Crescent Island** on October 12, 2026, is an inference-first GPU (Xe3P architecture, 1.3 PFLOPS FP8, 350W, 160GB LPDDR5X), sampling to partners with general availability likely in 2027 and no published benchmarks. Status: *sampling, two of the three challenger boxes (benchmarks, anchor customer) still empty.*

**The NVIDIA partnership products.** Two families are promised from the September 2025 partnership: custom **Xeon CPUs for NVIDIA's NVLink Fusion** systems (x86 hosts inside AI racks) and a consumer **x86 RTX SoC** (Intel CPU plus NVIDIA graphics; the codename "Serpent Lake" circulates in leaks — unconfirmed). The partnership was confirmed ongoing in May 2026. Status: *agreement stage — no product launched, none dated, as of October 30, 2026.*

**The supporting cast.** Advanced packaging (EMIB and Foveros, anchored at Fab 9 in New Mexico, with a glass-substrate collaboration with Lens Technology noted in the Q3 release) is the product Intel sells most credibly to others today. Altera (FPGAs) is 49%-owned after the Silver Lake sale. The edge/networking unit was kept and folded into the product groups, repositioned toward "physical AI." Read the portfolio as Tan's sentence: concentrate on CPUs, process, and packaging; borrow reach everywhere else.
