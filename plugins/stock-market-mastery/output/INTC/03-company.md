# Part One — Financials

Intel's financials describe a company healing at the operating line, financed by a coalition and one large equity raise, and carrying an accounting structure so unusual that its nine-month GAAP result must be explained before it can be read.

**Margins: the recovery gauge.** Gross margin is where process health shows up, and the trajectory is the turnaround's vital sign: from the low-30s at the 2024 trough to **43.0% GAAP / 44.1% non-GAAP in Q3 2026**, on revenue of $16.513B (+21%). Q4 is guided back down to 37.1%/38.0% — mix (client volume falling), deleverage, and ~200bp from the Fab 28 incident — a guided step-down that says more about cyclicality than about the process story. Operating income was $2.522B GAAP in Q3 (15.3% margin; 19.6% non-GAAP), with the segment split of Part One's company chapter: products strongly positive, Foundry −$1.853B.

**The escrow paradox.** Intel's nine-month 2026 net result is **−$11.609B** — in a period when its stock roughly doubled. The mechanism: part of the U.S. government's 2025 investment sits in escrow as an equity-linked instrument whose accounting value rises with Intel's share price; when the stock rises, Intel books a **non-cash mark-to-market loss** — $12.614B over the nine months — and when it falls, a gain. GAAP EPS over any period containing these marks is not a performance measure; trailing P/E is not meaningful, and this book values Intel on operating lines, cash flow, and forward expectations instead. Q3's own GAAP net income of $3.320B (EPS $0.60; non-GAAP $0.52) also needs its register: +$330M of equity gains, +$1.130B of interest and other income, +$120M of escrow mark *gains* — roughly $1.58B of non-operating tailwind inside the headline. Symmetric discipline, as always: adjust in both directions, then judge the operating core.

**Cash flow and funding.** Q3 operating cash flow was **$5.082B** against $4.219B of gross capex — standard free cash flow ≈ +$0.86B; Intel's own "adjusted FCF" (which adds partner contributions per its reconciliation) printed +$1.639B. The turnaround's funding stack, in the order it was built: operations first, asset actions second (Altera's sale), the 2025 strategic stakes third (government, NVIDIA, SoftBank — ~$15.9B combined at $20–23 per share), and the August 2026 public offering fourth (~$22.6B net, into strength). Intel is *fundable* and increasingly cash-generative at the operating line; it is not yet *self-funding* through the fab investment hump.

**The three-number card.** Liquidity **$54.005B** (cash $21.846B + short-term investments $32.159B); total debt **$50.134B**; capex commitments **$19.6B** (10-Q). Roughly neutral net cash, with a heavy obligation stack behind it — workable if operating cash flow keeps recovering, which is the conditional the whole credit story hangs on. And the share count records the rescue's price: **5,310M shares issued**, roughly a billion more than the pre-rescue register — every future earning will be divided by that larger number.

> **Source:** Intel Q3 2026 earnings release · 2026-10-29 (margins, cash flow, liquidity, debt); Form 10-Q Q3 2026 · filed 2026-10-30 (escrow accounting, commitments, share count, nine-month results).
