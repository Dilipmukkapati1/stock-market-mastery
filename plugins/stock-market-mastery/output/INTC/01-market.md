# Part One — Management

Intel's management question is unusually stark: is Lip-Bu Tan's team executing a credible industrial turnaround, or narrating one? The evidence through October 2026 supports the former — with the decisive test still ahead of them.

**The CEO and the reset.** Tan became CEO in March 2025, inheriting a company that had lost process leadership, product momentum, and the market's trust. His sequence was disciplined and legible: first cost and focus — roughly a quarter of the workforce (headcount from ~108,900 to ~81,100), the Germany and Poland fab projects cancelled (July 2025), capex brought under explicit discipline; then, in 2026, re-acceleration once the cost base and the 18A ramp could carry it — a capex guide above $20B and a ~$23 billion equity offering completed in August 2026 ($22.617B net proceeds per the cash-flow statement), issued into stock strength rather than weakness. CFO David Zinsner has been the reset's second voice, and his Q3 candor — a PC slowdown lasting "at least through H1 2027" — typifies the regime: this management guides down in plain language when it sees weakness, a credibility asset that compounds.

**The governance coalition.** Intel's register is unlike any peer's. The **U.S. government** holds equity from its August 2025 investment ($8.9B for 433.3M shares at $20.47, funded by CHIPS and Secure Enclave money; ~159M shares in escrow; a warrant over 240.5M more shares at $20 that pays only if Intel's ownership of Foundry falls below 51% — an anti-breakup device); it takes **no board seat**, and dilution from the 2026 offering has taken the stake to roughly 8%. **NVIDIA** ($5B at $23.28, closed December 26, 2025) and **SoftBank** ($2B at $23.00, closed September 26, 2025; no board seat, no purchase commitment) complete a coalition of strategic holders whose presence argues that Intel's survival as an integrated manufacturer is now several powerful parties' stated interest. Management's task is to convert that political and strategic capital into the one thing it cannot substitute for: customers.

**The gate — management's own falsifier.** Tan has stated, and the 10-Q reflects, that Intel will invest in the 14A generation **only with a significant external customer committed**, and may pause or discontinue it otherwise. A CEO who publishes his kill-switch gives analysts their monitoring framework: the turnaround is, by management's own construction, a sequence of verifiable claims — 18A in high-volume production (delivered, January 2026), yields and costs improving (directionally claimed, not disclosed), and an external foundry customer of consequence (not yet delivered). Grade the claims in that order and the management verdict through Q3 2026 is: cost reset real, product engine stabilized, the decisive commercial proof still outstanding — which is precisely what the stock's October repricing said (Part Two).

> **Source:** Intel deal disclosures (Forms 8-K) · August–December 2025; Intel Q3 2026 earnings release and call · 2026-10-29; Form 10-Q Q3 2026 · filed 2026-10-30 (offering proceeds, commitments, gating language as reported).
