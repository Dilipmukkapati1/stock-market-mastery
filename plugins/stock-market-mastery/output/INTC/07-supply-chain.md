# Part Two — Deep Research I: The Turnaround in Depth

## Two industries, mirror images

Intel defends one structure and attacks another. In **x86 CPUs** it remains the incumbent: Mercury Research (Q2 2026, the latest published — several circulating "Q3" tables are actually 2025 data) gives Intel **65.5% of x86 server units** against AMD's 34.5%, and roughly 60–70% of client units. But the money tells the sharper story: AMD's server *revenue* share hit a record **46.2%** (Q1 2026) — an implied average-selling-price advantage of roughly 1.6× (book arithmetic from Mercury's figures), meaning AMD owns the expensive half of the market while Intel owns the voluminous half. Around the duopoly, **Arm holds 13.6% of server shipments** and the hyperscalers' own silicon programs; x86's compatibility moat erodes workload by workload, greenfield first. In **foundry**, the mirror flips: TSMC holds ~70% of global merchant revenue with the neutrality moat Intel structurally cannot copy (it competes with its would-be customers), and Intel's external foundry revenue annualizes to roughly $1.2–1.6B — under 1% of the leader's scale. Intel's strategy must therefore win a defense (CPUs, against AMD inside the architecture and Arm around it) and an offense (foundry, against the strongest incumbent in the industry) simultaneously, with the defense's cash funding the offense. That is the turnaround's actual shape.

## The economics of the staple

The staple's arithmetic, from the segment tables: in FY2025 the product groups earned roughly $12.7B of operating income (client $9.3B, DCAI $3.4B) while Foundry lost $10.3B — near-total consumption. Through 2026 the balance improved: Q3's products earned $5.3B against Foundry's $1.85B loss, and gross margin recovered from the low-30s trough to 43–44%. Management's foundry trajectory — losses narrowing as 18A volume internalizes, with a break-even path discussed toward late 2028 (as reported from company commentary) — requires three things to compound at once: product margins holding (DCAI mix is doing this today), internal wafer volume rising (Panther Lake and Clearwater Forest ramps), and the external revenue line converting from non-recurring to structural. The first two are visible in the prints. The third is the empty chair of Part One's Customers chapter — and it is the difference between a foundry that is a *cost center with a story* and one that is a business.

## The Q3 2026 event, dissected

*The beat:* revenue $16.513B (+21%), DCAI +78% (best in fifteen years), gross margin 43.0/44.1%, operating income $2.522B — ahead of the company's own prior outlook on data-center strength. *The register:* GAAP net income of $3.320B carried ~$1.58B of non-operating tailwinds (+$330M equity gains, +$1,130M interest and other, +$120M escrow mark gains), while the nine-month GAAP result remained −$11.609B on the escrow mechanism's $12.614B of non-cash losses — the same accounts, opposite distortions, one discipline: itemize, sign, classify, then judge operations (15.3% GAAP operating margin; 19.6% non-GAAP). *The guide:* Q4 revenue $15–16B against ~$17.1B consensus (a ≈9% gap at midpoint), gross margin 37.1/38.0%, EPS $0.10/$0.25 against ~$0.43 — with client down over 20% sequentially, the PC slowdown guided through H1 2027, and ~$300M/~200bp of the guide consumed by the October 14 Fab 28 explosion in Kiryat Gat, a fatal incident at a production fab whose financial shadow (scrapped work-in-process, requalification, fixed-cost deleverage) is the textbook mechanics of operational risk. *The verdict:* the stock fell **28.58% on October 30** ($92.07 → $65.76; market cap ≈ $349B), and the analyst cascade — 17 target cuts in a day: BofA Buy → Neutral ($145 → $100, "not enough traction with external foundry customers," EPS power pushed to 2030), Roth Buy → Neutral ($120 → $70), Jefferies ($120 → $85), Baird ($125 → $83), DA Davidson ($100 → $70) — clustered on one broken assumption, and it was not the quarter, nor even the guide: it was the foundry customer timeline. JPMorgan had upgraded the stock on October 19 (to Neutral, $85) on foundry-execution hope; Citi's summary — "strong beat, weak outlook" — is the print in four words. At a trajectory stock, the guide is the earnings, and the customer ledger is the guide behind the guide.

## What must be true

For the turnaround to complete on anything like its current valuation (~$349B, roughly 6× sales at October 30 — book arithmetic — against a trailing P/E that is not meaningful), the checkable claims are: a **binding volume customer** for 18A-P or 14A inside the H2-2026–2027 window Tan's gate implies; **yield and cost curves** on 18A that keep gross margin climbing through the client downturn toward the high-40s; **Xeon share defense** — Diamond Rapids on time against EPYC's next generations, holding server revenue share near half; a **foundry loss trajectory** visibly narrowing toward the late-2028 break-even path; and **no further dilution** of consequence — the August 2026 raise must be the last rescue-scale issuance. Each is falsifiable in filings. That falsifiability is the bull case's best feature and the bear case's best weapon, and Part Two's second chapter takes up the industrial and competitive evidence for each.
