# Part Two — Deep Research II: Foundry, Competition, Valuation and Risks

## The process program in depth

Intel's industrial bet is a three-generation ladder, each rung gated by the last. **18A** — the comeback node, with RibbonFET transistors and PowerVia backside power delivery — reached high-volume manufacturing at Fab 52 (Arizona) in January 2026 and carries Panther Lake and the Clearwater Forest server part. Its economics are improving per management (costs down ~40% since Q1; yields improving double-digits in Q3, absolute level undisclosed). **18A-P**, the performance variant, entered risk production in Q2 2026 with internal enablement targeted by end-2026; it carries Nova Lake and Diamond Rapids, and it is the node attached to the reported Apple interest. **14A** is the conditional generation: 0.9 PDK released October 2026, risk production targeted H2 2027, high-volume 2028 — and, per Tan's stated rule reflected in the 10-Q, it proceeds only with a significant external customer, with pause or discontinuation the stated alternative. On the technology merits, 18A-class silicon is genuinely competitive with TSMC's N2 generation — with PowerVia roughly a year ahead of TSMC's A16 backside-power variant (as assessed in trade-press analysis) — which is precisely why the contest has moved from "can Intel build it?" to "will anyone commit to buying it?" The fab map expresses the same triage: Arizona is the leading-edge center (Fab 52 in HVM; Fab 62 installing tools from October 2026 for ~2028); New Mexico (Fab 9) anchors advanced packaging — EMIB/Foveros, plus the Lens Technology glass-substrate collaboration; **Ohio is a 2030s asset** (production 2030–31, Mod 2 in 2032; first structural truss raised October 21, 2026); Germany and Poland are cancelled. Capex commitments stand at $19.6B (10-Q) — the ladder is expensive to stand still on.

## The supply chain Intel owns — and the one it doesn't

Owning fabs concentrates a particular risk set. **ASML** is the canonical single source: EUV and High-NA EUV lithography come from one Dutch supplier, and Intel's early High-NA adoption (development systems in Oregon) is a double edge — learning-curve lead, plus concentrated exposure to an immature tool ecosystem its rivals can let Intel debug. Materials and substrates run through a concentrated, largely Japanese supplier base. **Geography** stopped being theoretical on October 14, 2026, when the fatal Fab 28 explosion in Kiryat Gat, Israel, converted directly into guidance (−$300M revenue, ~200bp gross margin in Q4): leading-edge wafers cannot be re-routed between fabs in the short term, so single-site nodes are concentrations, not conveniences. **China** is three channels at once: demand ($12.694B, ≈24% of FY2025 revenue, down from ~29% — the largest market), substitution (state-favored domestic CPU and Arm/RISC-V programs aimed at displacing x86), and operations (Asian assembly/test exposure, plus the January 2026 25% Section 232 tariff development on certain chip imports, as reported). The same United States government that constrains Intel's largest market is its largest strategic shareholder — a loop no risk-factor section fully captures, and the correct frame for reading every Intel policy headline.

## Competition

**AMD** is the inside-the-architecture rival: record server revenue share (46.2%, Q1 2026, Mercury), the high-ASP end of both server and client, and roadmaps (EPYC Venice/Verano) timed against Intel's Diamond Rapids. **Arm and its licensees** — Ampere, and the hyperscalers' in-house programs — press the flank: 13.6% of server shipments and growing, winning greenfield and AI-adjacent workloads on performance-per-watt. **Apple** owns the premium client reference point. **TSMC** is the foundry rival and, awkwardly, also Intel's supplier for selected products under IDM 2.0 — a relationship that gives Intel product competitiveness today while funding the incumbent it must displace tomorrow. **NVIDIA** is partner, shareholder, and the ecosystem standard Intel's accelerator ambitions must route around (the NVLink Fusion Xeon program is exactly that routing). The competitive summary is uncomfortable and clarifying: Intel is the only company in the industry fighting on all five fronts at once, which is either the argument for its strategic indispensability or the explanation of its economics — depending on the foundry customer's signature.

## Valuation

Intel's headline multiples are broken in instructive ways. **Trailing P/E is not meaningful**: the TTM GAAP result carries the escrow mechanism's non-cash marks (nine-month net −$11.609B in a rising stock). **Price-to-sales ≈ 6×** at the October 30 close ($65.76; market cap ≈ $349B on 5,310M shares — book arithmetic) sits near prior cycle-peak territory for Intel and roughly double its decline-era levels: the market is paying today for a mid-recovery earnings structure. **Forward estimates** are the real battleground — BofA's post-Q3 note framed 2030 EPS power of $5–6, which is an admission that the valuation case is a 2028–2030 case; discounted back through a 3.75–4.00% policy-rate world, small changes in the assumed terminal year move the present value violently, as the year's price path demonstrated (52-week range **$32.89–$142.35**; +~78% year-to-date even after the crash, per Finnhub). Post-crash analyst targets span roughly **$70–$100** — a dispersion that is itself the valuation data: this is a security whose value is a probability distribution over industrial outcomes, not a discounted cash flow with a rounding error.

## Risks and the two cases

**The bear case** (the October 30 downgrade cluster): the external customer never signs at volume; 14A pauses and the foundry narrative collapses back to an internal cost center losing $7–10B a year; 18A's undisclosed yields plateau below TSMC's; the client downturn deepens; Diamond Rapids slips against EPYC; and the bill for the attempt — ~1B shares of dilution, $50B of debt, $19.6B of commitments — remains after the story ends. **The bull case** (JPMorgan's October upgrade logic, Cestrian's post-crash accumulation stance, both as reported): 18A execution is real and compounding; a Tesla-class or Apple-class signature converts the gate into a launch; packaging becomes the wedge business; DCAI's attach-rate moment funds the wait; and the only American leading-edge manufacturer, backed by its government and by NVIDIA's ecosystem alike, re-rates from ~6× sales on mid-cycle earnings power several times today's. **What would change the picture**, in checkable form: any binding external foundry commitment (an 8-K, not a quote); foundry external revenue converting to recurring, multi-customer form in the segment note; gross margin holding the mid-40s through the client trough; Diamond Rapids shipping on schedule; and the escrow overhang's resolution terms when the government's position is finalized. Until the first of those prints, Intel remains what Q3 proved it to be: a real operational recovery priced as an industrial option — and options, as October 30 demonstrated, reprice without asking permission.
