# Part One · Section 4 — Products

**The short version:** Four product families, one strategy — be the #2 (and increasingly co-#1) compute supplier everywhere NVIDIA and Intel play.

**EPYC server CPUs.** 5th-gen "Turin" (Zen 5) is the current platform; 6th-gen "Venice" (Zen 6, TSMC 2nm) is the 2026 platform paired with Helios. EPYC is the quiet backbone of AMD's profit story.

**Instinct AI accelerators.** MI300X (192GB HBM3) → MI325X (256GB) → **MI350X/MI355X** (CDNA 4, 288GB HBM3E, ~4,600 FP8 TFLOPS class) ramping through 2026 → **MI450 series** (CDNA 5, 2nm, up to 432GB HBM4) at the heart of **Helios**, AMD's rack-scale AI system (GPU + EPYC Venice + Pensando networking in one rack) shipping from late 2026. AMD has committed to an **annual cadence** of accelerators and racks — matching NVIDIA's rhythm. MI430X serves HPC; MI440X enterprise inference.

**Client.** Ryzen CPUs and APUs for desktops, laptops, and handhelds — record market share (see Section 5).

**Gaming & Embedded.** Radeon GPUs and the semi-custom SoCs inside PlayStation and Xbox; Xilinx-derived FPGAs and adaptive SoCs for industrial, automotive, aerospace, and test equipment — steady, slower-growing, cash-generative.

**The software layer.** **ROCm** is AMD's answer to NVIDIA's CUDA — the stack developers actually program against. Its quality is assessed in the next section; it is the most debated part of the whole company.

> **Source:** AMD product/launch disclosures 2025–2026 · AMD earnings releases · press reporting on Helios/MI450 · 2026
