# Part Two · Deep Research 3 — The GW Deals, Customers & Risks

## Anatomy of a gigawatt deal

The OpenAI agreement (Oct 2025) is the template: OpenAI deploys **up to 6 GW** of Instinct GPUs across multiple generations, starting with 1 GW of MI450 in 2H 2026. In exchange for the commitment, AMD issued OpenAI a **warrant for up to 160M shares at $0.01 each**, vesting in tranches tied to (a) GW deployment milestones and (b) escalating AMD share-price targets — the final tranche requires **$600/share**. Meta's February 2026 deal mirrors it: up to 6 GW, another 160M-share penny warrant. Anthropic (2026) adds up to 2 GW of Helios from 1H 2027, with AMD investing up to $5B *into* Anthropic.

**The numbers that matter:**

- Combined OpenAI + Meta warrants: **320M shares ≈ 19.6% potential dilution** against 1.632B shares outstanding.
- As of Jun 27, 2026, **zero** OpenAI warrant shares had vested — the dilution is contingent on AMD actually shipping gigawatts *and* the stock performing.
- "Up to" GW figures are commitment frameworks with conditions — **not booked backlog**. Revenue arrives rack by rack, starting late 2026.

> **Source:** Motley Fool · warrant analysis · 2026-10-05 · AMD 10-Q (vesting status) · company announcements Oct 2025 / Feb 2026

**The circularity debate.** Critics note the structure's optics: AMD gives customers equity worth billions at full vesting; customers commit to buy AMD hardware; the stock rises on the announcements, making the warrants valuable — each side's gain partly denominated in the other's paper. Bulls counter that the warrants only pay out if AMD *delivers product and the stock performs* — the $600 final tranche aligns OpenAI/Meta with AMD's execution, and anchor-tenant commitments are how a #2 supplier buys its way into a monopolist's market. Both readings are live; October 2026's ~19% drawdown was partly attributed to worries that OpenAI's revenue pace could slow its 6 GW deployment — the first market test of the structure's fragility.

## Risk register

1. **China export controls (demonstrated, costly).** MI308 restrictions produced ~**$800M of inventory and related charges in Q2 2025**; FY2025 net charges ~$440M after a Q4 reserve release when licenses allowed ~$390M of China MI308 revenue. Policy can reverse in either direction with a headline — an un-modelable, recurring risk.
2. **Execution & supply.** Helios/MI450 is supply-constrained (HBM memory, TSMC CoWoS packaging). Lisa Su toured Asia in Oct 2026 securing capacity and named supply as the gating item; AMD has publicly rebutted MI455X delay rumors, insisting Helios is on target for 2H 2026. The entire 2027 "data center doubles" story rides on this ramp.
3. **CUDA moat.** If training stays on NVIDIA and inference commoditizes, AMD's accelerator window narrows to price-sensitive serving — lower margins, lower multiple.
4. **Customer concentration & deal quality.** Forward growth is concentrated in 3–4 counterparties whose commitments are conditional. A single partner's capex pause (see: October's OpenAI worries) moves AMD's numbers.
5. **Warrant dilution.** Up to ~19.6% contingent share issuance at $0.01 — real dilution if the deals succeed (the "good problem" version), worth modeling into per-share math either way.
6. **Gaming cycle.** Semi-custom fades until next-gen consoles (rumored 2027, unconfirmed); Gaming was −31% YoY in Q2 2026.
7. **Acquisition risk.** The pending **~$8.2B all-stock World Labs** deal (AI research lab, announced Sep 2026) is a large, dilutive bet on world-models research paying off inside a chip company — integration and payoff are unproven.
8. **TSMC dependence.** All leading-edge supply runs through one foundry in Taiwan — shared with the whole industry, but AMD has no fallback.

> **Source:** AMD filings/releases (MI308 charges) · press reporting Oct 2026 (supply, delay rebuttal, drawdown attribution)
