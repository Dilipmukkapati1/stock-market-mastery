## Rival by rival

**Scale AI — the wounded giant.** The incumbent. In June 2025 Meta bought 49% for $14.3B and took founder Alexandr Wang in-house. The strategic cost was immediate: OpenAI, Google, and xAI cut ties rather than feed a Meta-owned vendor. Scale kept the biggest balance sheet in the industry but lost its neutrality — and the work it lost **split to Surge and Mercor**. Scale is the proof that in this market, *who owns you* is a product feature.

**Surge AI — the disciplined threat.** Founded by Edwin Chen (ex-Google/Meta), bootstrapped to >$1B reported revenue and profitability on ~$25M raised, now reportedly raising $1B at $15B+. Surge inherited much of the frontier human-feedback business after the Meta–Scale deal. It is Mercor's most dangerous rival precisely because it doesn't need the growth story to be true — it's already profitable, so it can compete on price and patience indefinitely.

**Turing — the profitable comp.** $2.2B valuation on $300M disclosed revenue (7.3x), profitable, 4M+ expert network, same named lab customers. Turing is what Mercor's financials look like with the volume turned down and the discipline turned up — and its multiple is the market's anchor for what this business is worth *without* hypergrowth.

**The rest.** Invisible Technologies (~$410M est. revenue), Snorkel AI (~$375M est.), Outlier, micro1 — credible second tier. Upwork and Andela are generalist talent marketplaces with low overlap in the expert-data segment.

## Mercor's position

Mercor's edge is the combination rivals can't copy outright: the AI-interview vetting engine at 5M-profile scale, premium expert pay ($85+/hr) attracting top-tier supply, and independence from every lab. Its vulnerabilities mirror the same list: the vetting format is replicable, experts multi-home across platforms (supply isn't exclusive), and independence means no deep-pocketed parent if growth stalls. The Deeptune acquisition (evals) is the clearest attempt to build something rivals can't simply hire their way into.
