# Chapter 9 — The Investment Case

*Educational summary, not investment advice. No IPO is announced; there is no public price, no 409A disclosure, and no confirmed secondary market for Mercor shares.*

## The bull case

1. **The ramp is real, even on conservative numbers.** ~$760M gross in 2025 (reported actual) and $614M in H1 2026 — with the $4M/day payout figure independently corroborating the $2B gross run rate.
2. **It sits on the richest demand pool in AI.** Frontier labs spend ~$1B/year each on human data, and the spend is shifting toward exactly what Mercor sells: vetted experts, not bulk labeling.
3. **Marketplace economics.** ~$1.75M net revenue per employee, reported free-cash-flow profitability, and only ~$485.6M raised to get here — capital efficiency most AI startups can't claim.
4. **The priced multiple has already compressed** from ~76x net (Series B) to ~14x net today through growth alone. Time has been the investor's friend so far.
5. **Optionality.** Deeptune/APEX moves Mercor from selling hours toward owning measurement — the layer of the stack with the best claim to durability.

## The bear case

1. **Concentration with a demonstrated failure.** Meta paused everything in April 2026. The top-customer revenue share is undisclosed, and the same event can recur with any lab.
2. **The take rate is the whole margin.** ~35% today; profitable rivals (Surge, Turing) can compress it, and a 10-point slip cuts net revenue ~30% at flat volume.
3. **Unresolved litigation aimed at the core product.** Five class actions; the FCRA/BIPA theories challenge the AI-interview model itself, not just security practices.
4. **The $20B anchor is vapor until it closes.** Reported in talks since July 2026, unclosed as of October — and at ~28x net it would price in perfection.
5. **Nothing is audited.** Every number in this book is disclosed-by-choice, reported, or estimated. The two revenue tellings (Sep vs Oct 2025) don't even agree with each other.

## What to watch

| Signal | Bullish version | Bearish version |
|---|---|---|
| $20B round | Closes at/near $20B with broad syndicate | Quietly dies or closes lower (down-signal) |
| Customer base | A 6th–7th major buyer disclosed; Meta confirmed resumed | Another lab pause; insourcing announcements |
| Take rate | Holds ~35% as volume grows | Slips toward 25% under rival pressure |
| Litigation | Suits dismissed/settled cheaply | FCRA/BIPA claims survive motions to dismiss |
| Revenue quality | Audited or actuals-based reporting begins | Run-rate-only headlines continue |
| Evals business | APEX adopted as a lab standard | Deeptune stays a side project |

**The bottom line.** Mercor is a genuinely exceptional growth story attached to genuinely exceptional risks: a concentrated customer base that has already demonstrated it can vanish, a margin that lives in a single negotiable percentage, and litigation aimed at its core product — all priced at $10B with no audited numbers underneath. The company deserves the attention; the valuation deserves the scrutiny. Both statements are true at once, and holding them at once is the entire skill of pre-IPO analysis.
