# Chapter 2 — What Mercor Sells

## The product, plainly

Mercor sells **vetted human expertise, by the hour, to AI labs**. A frontier lab training a model needs thousands of hours of expert work: a doctor grading medical answers, a lawyer writing legal-reasoning examples, an engineer producing code with explanations. Mercor supplies that labor through one integrated service:

1. **Vetting.** Candidates upload a resume and take a ~20-minute AI video interview with algorithmic scoring — no human recruiter. This is how the 5M+ profile pool gets built.
2. **Matching.** Labs post projects; Mercor matches experts by specialty and track record.
3. **Contracting & payment.** Mercor handles global contractor agreements and pays experts — ~$4M/day at the last company-disclosed figure — so labs get one vendor, one invoice.

The work itself is called **post-training**: RLHF (humans ranking model outputs), reasoning-trace generation (experts showing their work so models learn to reason), rubric creation, and model evaluations.

## Who buys, and how much they spend

The buyers are the frontier AI labs — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, Nvidia are the names in reporting — plus, per Mercor's own claim, "6 of the Magnificent Seven." Industry reporting puts each major lab's spend on human data at roughly **$1B per year**: this is a small number of buyers spending enormous sums, which is both Mercor's opportunity and its central risk (Chapter 7).

## The demand shift Mercor rode

The AI data market used to mean bulk labeling — thousands of low-cost workers tagging images. The money has moved to **expert annotation**: as models got smarter, training them required people smarter than the task. Market estimates for data labeling run from ~$4–8B in 2025 toward $17–33B by the early 2030s (17–28% CAGR depending on the firm) — but the segment Mercor serves, expert post-training for frontier labs, is where the growth and the margins are.

> **Source:** Emergen Research / Dataintelo / Grand View Research · market estimates · 2026 (directional; definitions differ by firm)

## The expansion: from hours to measurement

In July 2026 Mercor acquired **Deeptune**, which builds simulated software environments for training AI agents, and has been pushing into **evaluations and benchmarks (APEX)**. Strategically, this is a move up the value chain: expert *hours* are a commodity input any rival can supply; *measurement* — being the standard by which labs judge their agents — is stickier and higher-margin. Whether APEX becomes a standard is unproven, but the direction tells you where Mercor thinks the durable money is.
