# Chapter 3 — The Company Story

## Three founders, one debate team

Mercor was founded in January 2023 by **Brendan Foody, Adarsh Hiremath, and Surya Midha** — teammates on Bellarmine College Prep's nationally ranked speech-and-debate team in San Jose, then college sophomores (Foody and Midha at Georgetown, Hiremath at Harvard). They won **Thiel Fellowships in March 2024** and dropped out permanently. Forbes reported in March 2026 that each founder owns roughly **22%** of the company — an unusually large combined stake this late, meaning the founders kept control through four funding rounds — and that the Series C made all three among the world's youngest self-made billionaires.

> **Source:** Contrary Research · founding story · https://research.contrary.com/company/mercor
> **Source:** Forbes · Brendan Foody profile · updated 2026-03-10 (reported)

## Act one: recruiting (2023)

Mercor launched as an AI-driven marketplace matching freelance software engineers — largely in India — with US startups. The first client paid **$500/week**, Mercor keeping ~30%. Matching was done manually over WhatsApp and Google Sheets from dorm rooms, and the business bootstrapped to roughly **$1M in annualized revenue in 2023**. To scale vetting beyond what three students could do by hand, they built the AI interviewer — the 20-minute structured video assessment that is still the product's core.

## Act two: the pivot (2024–2025)

The turning point was inbound: **Scale AI approached Mercor** looking for specialized coders for AI training data. That revealed the real market — frontier labs would pay premium rates for vetted experts doing post-training work, and Mercor's vetting engine was exactly the supply machine for it. Through 2024–2025 Mercor reoriented from "hire engineers" to "supply expertise to labs": RLHF, reasoning traces, rubrics, evaluations.

Note what kind of pivot this was: the *technology stayed the same* (AI vetting and matching); the *customer changed* (startups → AI labs). Demand-led pivots like this de-risk product-market fit — the buyer pulled the company in — but they concentrate risk in that buyer's budget cycle, which is Chapter 7's whole subject.
