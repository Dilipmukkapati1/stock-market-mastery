# Chapter 7 — Customers & Concentration

## The customer list

Mercor claims to work with **"the top five AI labs"** and **"6 of the Magnificent Seven."** Named in reporting: **OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, Nvidia**. No customer-by-customer revenue split has ever been disclosed — and that absence is the single biggest information gap in the entire Mercor story, because everything about this company's durability depends on how revenue is distributed across a handful of buyers each spending ~$1B/year on human data.

> **Source:** Mercor company claim · 2026 · customer names via press reporting (okara.ai, valueaddvc.com)

## The Meta pause — a case study in concentration

In March 2026, Mercor suffered a major data breach (full detail in Chapter 8). In **April 2026, Meta paused all Mercor contracts**. Read that sentence the way an investor should: a top-tier customer — plausibly representing a double-digit share of revenue — went to zero, not because of price or quality of work, but because of a *trust* failure in an adjacent system. Industry commentary has noted that "most of the revenue comes from a handful of AI labs paying for what is effectively a permanent talent contract" — permanent, that is, until the day it isn't.

> **Source:** yespress.io · "Mercor: The Recruiter for the Machines" · 2026 · research fact pack

**What we don't know:** whether Meta has resumed, at what volume, or what the pause cost in revenue. The reported H1 2026 and mid-2026 growth figures postdate the pause, which suggests either resumption, backfill from other labs, or both — but "suggests" is doing heavy lifting. This is the first question any serious diligence process would ask, and it has no public answer.

## Why labs concentrate spend — and why that's fragile

Labs prefer few vendors: security review is expensive, data pipelines integrate deeply, and quality calibration takes months. That stickiness protects incumbents — until the trigger isn't commercial. The realistic churn triggers in this market are: (1) **trust events** (the Meta precedent), (2) **capture events** (the Scale precedent — a vendor taking a rival lab's money), (3) **insourcing** (labs building internal expert networks as spend justifies it), and (4) **price** (Surge or Turing offering the same experts cheaper). Mercor has already been hit by #1; #2 disciplines its investor choices (note the reported Nvidia interest in the $20B round — a customer-investor is exactly the Scale pattern, at smaller scale).

## The diversification hedge

Two hedges are visible. First, **breadth within the lab set**: five-plus major buyers is concentrated but not single-threaded — losing one hurts badly without being fatal at current scale. Second, the **Deeptune/evals expansion**: benchmarks and agent-training environments sell a different product (measurement, not hours) to potentially a wider buyer set. Neither hedge is proven. The honest summary: Mercor's revenue is a concentrated bet on frontier-lab training budgets, and its growth rate and its risk come from the same source.
