# Chapter 4 — Financials I: The Revenue Ramp

Mercor reports no audited financials, so this chapter reconstructs its revenue from every disclosed and reported figure available — and cross-checks them against each other, because in private-company analysis the cross-checks are where the truth hides.

## The revenue timeline

| Date | Figure | Type | Attribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | ~$1M annualized | Run rate | Reported (early history) |
| Feb 2025 | $75M annualized | Run rate | Press-reported (TechCrunch) |
| Mar 2025 | $100M | Run rate | Press-reported |
| Sep 2025 | $500M annualized | Run rate | **Company-disclosed** (CEO: "$1M → $500M in 17 months") |
| Oct 2025 | ~$400M | Run rate at Series C | CEO's telling (reported) |
| End 2025 | ~$760M full-year | **Gross revenue** | Press-reported |
| Early 2026 | $1B annualized | Run rate | Company-claimed |
| H1 2026 | $614M | **Gross revenue** | Sacra estimate |
| Jun–Jul 2026 | $2B annualized | Gross run rate | Press-reported (The Information, TechCrunch) |

> **Source:** The Information · Jun 2026 · TechCrunch · Jul 2026 · Sacra · https://sacra.com/c/mercor/

**Read this table like an analyst, not a fan.** Three things jump out:

1. **Run rates vs. actuals.** Most figures are *run rates* — one strong period multiplied by 12. The two *actuals* (full-year 2025 gross ~$760M; H1 2026 gross $614M) imply H1 2026 annualizes to ~$1.23B — well below the "$2B run rate" headline. Both can be true (the run rate annualizes the best recent month), but the gap tells you the headline is the most flattering version of the truth.
2. **An internal inconsistency.** September 2025 was company-disclosed as a $500M run rate, yet the CEO's own telling put the Series C (October 2025) at ~$400M. Different definitions, different months, or optimistic rounding — unresolvable from outside, and exactly the kind of discrepancy to note rather than smooth over.
3. **The growth itself is real.** Even using only the conservative actuals — ~$760M gross in 2025, $614M in H1 2026 alone — this is one of the fastest revenue ramps ever recorded for a private company. Skepticism about the *headline* shouldn't become denial of the *trajectory*.

## The gross-to-net bridge (the most important math in this book)

Mercor's revenue is **gross payment volume**: what labs pay *through* the platform. Contractors receive an estimated 60–70%; Mercor keeps a **take rate estimated at 30–40%** (Sacra). Valuation is priced on what the company *keeps*:

| Gross run rate | Take rate | = Net revenue (kept) |
|---|---|---|
| $2B | 30% | $600M |
| $2B | 35% | $700M |
| $2B | 40% | $800M |

**Cross-check with payouts.** Mercor discloses ~$4M/day in expert payouts. Annualized: $4M × 365 ≈ **$1.46B/year paid out**. If payouts are 65% of gross, implied gross ≈ $1.46B ÷ 0.65 ≈ **$2.25B** — the same ballpark as the reported $2B run rate. The payout disclosure *corroborates* the gross revenue claim. This is how you validate a private company's numbers: find two independent figures that must reconcile, and check that they do.

> **Source:** Mercor newsroom · Sep 2026 · payout figure company-disclosed; take rate is a Sacra estimate

## Profitability — claims and evidence

- **"Profitable on a free-cash-flow basis as of early 2026"** — press-reported, no cash-flow statement published.
- **$6M profit in H1 2025** — Sacra-reported.
- Context: at an estimated ~$700M net revenue run rate with only ~400 employees, Mercor generates roughly **$1.75M of net revenue per employee** — extremely high (typical SaaS: $200–400K). Marketplace leverage is real: the contractors who deliver the service aren't employees. Plausible profitability is consistent with the structure; *verified* profitability doesn't exist without audited statements.

## Working capital — the hidden cost of growth

Mercor pays experts fast (daily payouts are the product's selling point to talent) but collects from enterprise labs on normal B2B terms. That gap — paying out ~$4M/day while waiting weeks to be paid — means **growth itself consumes cash**. The $485.6M raised isn't just war-chest; part of it funds the float. If growth stalls, the float unwinds in Mercor's favor; if a big lab pays late or disputes, the strain lands on Mercor first. None of this is disclosed in detail — flag it as a structural inference from the payout model, not a reported figure.
