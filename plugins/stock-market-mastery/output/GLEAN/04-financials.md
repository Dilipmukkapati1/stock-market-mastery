# Chapter 4 — Funding History & Reported Financials

**Learning objective:** Trace every funding round and every reported ARR figure for Glean, labeling each number as company-disclosed, press-reported, or analyst-estimated.

## Concept 1: The funding ladder

**Plain English.** Glean has raised roughly **$765–768M across six rounds** since 2019 — a steadier climb than the violent step-ups of newer AI startups, reflecting a company that compounded rather than exploded.

**Step-up math.** $1B → $2.2B (2.2x) → $4.6B (2.1x) → $7.2B (1.6x): decelerating multiples on an accelerating revenue base — the pattern of a company growing *into* its valuation rather than repricing on narrative. The CEO has said the raises were strategic ("flexibility to move faster"), not driven by capital need.

**2026 check.** No 2026 funding round has been found. One low-quality aggregator claimed a "$150M Series F in early 2026" — it contradicts all primary reporting (Series F closed June 2025); treat as erroneous, not as a second raise.

**Common mistake.** Adding the erroneous "2026 round" to the total. When one source contradicts five, the one is wrong until proven otherwise.

## Concept 3: The multiple, then and now

**Plain English.** At Series F, $7.2B on ~$100M ARR was **~72x revenue** — a multiple one commentator called "punchy." On today's ~$300M ARR, the same $7.2B is **~24x**. The valuation didn't move; the denominator tripled. This is the entire bull case in one fraction: *time plus growth heals an aggressive entry price.*

**Reference range.** Hypergrowth SaaS (>80% growth) has historically commanded 15–30x forward revenue in private markets; mature public SaaS sits at 5–12x. At ~24x on current ARR, Glean is priced for growth to continue — demanding, but no longer absurd.

**Worked example.** If ARR doubles again to $600M and the multiple compresses to 15x (mature-hypergrowth territory), implied value ≈ $9B — modest upside from $7.2B. If growth stalls at $350M and the multiple falls to 8x (Coveo-like), implied value ≈ $2.8B — a down round. The multiple is the whole game.

**Common mistake.** "72x was crazy, so $7.2B is still crazy." Multiples are snapshots; what matters is the multiple on *forward* revenue, which the company has been growing into.
