# Chapter 2 — The Enterprise Search & "Work AI" Industry

**Learning objective:** Describe the market Glean sells into — its size, its growth, and the structural force (AI assistants bundled by hyperscalers) that shapes every competitor's fate.

## Concept 1: What "enterprise search" became

**Plain English.** Enterprise search started as "Google for your company's internal data" — one box that finds documents across Slack, email, Drive, and wikis. The AI wave rebranded and expanded it into **"Work AI"**: search plus retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over company data, plus an AI assistant, plus agents that act on that data. Glean rode exactly this evolution: founded 2019 as enterprise search, repositioned 2024–2025 as a Work AI platform.

**Formal definition.** Work AI = enterprise software that indexes organizational knowledge (documents, messages, tickets, code) and exposes it through search, conversational assistants, and workflow agents — with access controls mirroring each user's permissions.

**Why it matters for Glean.** Glean's reported ~89% year-over-year ARR growth dwarfs the market's ~8–11%. That means Glean is *taking share* (or expanding what counts as the market via agents), not just riding a wave. Share capture is more impressive — and more fragile — than market growth.

**Reference range.** A 8–11% CAGR is roughly in line with overall enterprise software growth (~10%) — this is a mainstream software market, not a hypergrowth niche like AI data services.

**Common mistake.** Treating "enterprise search" and "AI agents" as the same market. Glean's agent push (Glean Agents, 1B agent actions targeted for end-2025) reaches into the much larger workflow-automation market — bigger prize, different competitors (ServiceNow, Microsoft).

## Concept 3: The bundling threat — the industry's permanent weather

**Plain English.** Microsoft Copilot comes with Microsoft 365; Google Gemini comes with Workspace. Enterprises already pay for those suites, so a bundled assistant is "free." Every independent Work AI vendor lives under this threat: why pay Glean per seat when Copilot is included?

**Glean's counter-evidence (reported).** Glean reached $300M ARR *alongside* massive Copilot/Gemini deployment — enterprises are paying for both, which suggests Glean does something the bundles don't (cross-app search across 100+ connectors, permissions-aware retrieval across the whole stack, model routing). The bull case isn't "bundling won't happen" — it's "bundling happened and Glean still grew."

> **Source:** Brief News · "Glean's AI Platform Surges to $300M Revenue" · 2026-09-02 · https://www.brief.news/2026/09/02/glean-surges-to-300m-revenue

**Common mistake.** "Microsoft will kill them." Bundled incumbents have killed many categories — but they've also failed to kill many others (Slack survived Teams for years; Datadog thrives alongside cloud-native monitoring). The question is always *what the independent does that the bundle can't*, not whether the bundle exists.

**DIY check.** Ask: "What would it cost Microsoft to replicate this — and would they bother?" Cross-vendor, permissions-aware search across a competitor's apps is structurally awkward for a bundler.

**Reading the table.** Two takeouts in one year (Moveworks $2.85B, Sana $1.05B) validate that large platforms will pay up for Work AI capability — that's strategic value under Glean's price. But Coveo at ~2x revenue shows what the *public market* pays a slower-growing, unprofitable search comp — the compression risk if Glean's growth slows.
