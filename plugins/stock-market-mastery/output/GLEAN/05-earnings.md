# Chapter 5 — Valuation Analysis

**Learning objective:** Compare Glean's priced valuation against competitors using consistent denominators, and build labeled bull/base/bear scenarios.

**Disclaimer for this chapter:** Educational illustration only. Not investment advice, not a price target.

## Concept 1: The comparable set — takeouts vs. the public comp

**Plain English.** Glean has the rare advantage of *two* recent takeouts in its exact category plus one public comp:

| Company | Valuation | Revenue basis | Implied multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glean | $7.2B (priced, Jun 2025) | $300M ARR (May 2026, reported) | **~24x** |
| Moveworks | $2.85B (ServiceNow takeout, Dec 2025) | ~$140M+ (2024, implied) | **~20x** |
| Sana | $1.05B (takeout, Nov 2025) | n/a | n/a (validates category) |
| Coveo (TSX: CVO) | ~$291M mkt cap (Aug 2026) | $151M TTM, loss-making | **~1.9x** |

> **Source:** MarketWireNews · ServiceNow/Moveworks close · Dec 2025 · https://marketwirenews.com/stock/now/news/servicenow-completes-acquisition-of-moveworks-8954958542146266.html
> **Source:** PitchBook · Coveo & Sana profiles · 2026

**Reading the table.** Moveworks at ~20x says strategic buyers pay roughly what Glean's investors paid — Glean's ~24x is in the same neighborhood, not an outlier. Coveo at ~2x says the *public market* pays very little for slower-growing, unprofitable search — that's the floor if growth breaks. The gap between 24x and 2x is the price of sustained hypergrowth.

**Reference range.** Strategic takeouts of high-growth SaaS: 10–25x revenue (wide; depends on growth and scarcity). Public mature SaaS: 4–10x. Glean sits at the top of the takeout band — fair if it stays the independent category leader, rich if not.

**Common mistake.** "Moveworks got 20x, so Glean deserves 24x+." Takeout multiples include a control premium (typically 20–40%) that a minority secondary purchase doesn't get. Strip the premium before comparing.

## Concept 2: Scenario thinking — bull, base, bear (all hypotheses)

- **Bull hypothesis.** Work AI becomes a budgeted enterprise category; Glean's agents drive net revenue retention above 130%; a hyperscaler or platform pays a strategic premium. Arithmetic: $600M ARR × 20x = ~$12B. *Assumes* bundling stays a non-fatal threat and growth stays above 60%.
- **Base hypothesis.** Growth moderates to 40–50% as the base scales; multiple compresses to 12–15x on forward revenue; no IPO before 2028. Arithmetic: $450M ARR × 13x ≈ ~$6B — roughly flat to the last round. *Assumes* no major customer loss and no pricing collapse.
- **Bear hypothesis.** Microsoft/Google bundles cross the "good enough" threshold; seat expansion stalls; growth drops under 25%. Arithmetic: $350M ARR × 6–8x (Coveo-plus-growth territory) ≈ $2–3B — a down round. *This is the multiple-compression scenario.*

**Why three scenarios.** Glean's valuation is almost entirely multiple, not earnings — and multiples are sentiment-driven. When the price is 24x revenue, the question is never "is it profitable" but "does growth justify the multiple for one more round."

**DIY check.** For each scenario write the *one fact* that would prove it wrong (e.g., bull dies if net revenue retention prints below 110%).

## Concept 3: What "no 2026 round" means

**Plain English.** Sixteen months without a raise, while reportedly cash-flow positive and growing ~89%, is a *choice* — the company doesn't need capital. That's strength. But it also means the $7.2B mark is aging: every quarter without a priced round, the valuation rests more on reported ARR and less on a negotiated price. Stale marks drift from reality in both directions.

**Common mistake.** "No new round means nothing's happening." For a cash-flow-positive company, no round is the bullish signal — dilution-free growth.
