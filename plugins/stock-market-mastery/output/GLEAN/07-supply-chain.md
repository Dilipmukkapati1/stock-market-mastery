# Chapter 7 — Risks: Bundling, Multiple & Trust

**Learning objective:** Price the downside. For a company valued at ~24x revenue with no audited filings, the risk section carries the analytical weight.

## Risk 1: Hyperscaler bundling — the permanent threat

**Plain English.** Microsoft Copilot ships with M365; Google Gemini ships with Workspace. Both are "free" inside contracts enterprises already pay for. Glean must win a separate budget line, every renewal, against an included alternative.

**Why it hasn't killed Glean (yet).** $300M ARR was built *while* Copilot/Gemini saturated the enterprise — customers pay for both, implying Glean's cross-app, permissions-aware search does something the bundles don't. But "hasn't yet" is not "can't ever": bundling improves every year, and the history of enterprise software is littered with best-of-breed vendors absorbed into suites.

**DIY check.** Ask: "What percentage of Glean's customers also pay for Copilot?" If high, Glean is a complement today — complements become redundant when the platform catches up.

## Risk 2: The multiple — 24x leaves no room for deceleration

**Plain English.** At ~24x current ARR, the price assumes hypergrowth continues. If growth decelerates from ~89% to ~30% — a normal maturation — the multiple can compress toward the 6–12x mature-SaaS band *faster than revenue grows*, and the valuation falls anyway. This is the classic growth-stock trap, and it needs no scandal: just gravity.

**Worked example (Chapter 4's math, repeated because it matters).** $350M ARR × 8x = $2.8B — below the last priced round — with revenue still *growing*. Valuation can fall while the business improves. Internalize that.

**Reference range.** Public high-growth SaaS drawdowns of 50–70% on growth deceleration are historically routine (2021–2023 cohort). Private marks just recognize it later.

## Risk 3: Permissions trust — the existential single point of failure

**Plain English.** Glean's entire enterprise value proposition rests on one promise: the AI never shows you what you can't already access. A single permissions bug — one confidential doc surfaced to the wrong employee — would be a trust catastrophe in exactly the buyer (security-conscious enterprise) Glean depends on.

**Mitigation, not guarantee.** The permissions-aware architecture is designed for this; there is no reported breach or incident. But absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — and unlike a churned customer, a trust incident is non-linear.

**Common mistake.** "No breach reported = secure." For a private company, you'd hear about it only if a customer or journalist surfaced it. Treat the risk as unpriced, not as zero.

## Risk 4: Model commoditization and cost structure

**Plain English.** Glean routes across 40+ LLMs and deliberately avoids premium frontier models where possible — smart cost control, but also a confession: the model layer is rented and interchangeable. If the intelligence is commoditized, what exactly is the customer paying 24x revenue for? The answer has to be the plumbing (permissions, graph, connectors) — see Chapter 6's moat scoring.

## Risk 5: Evidence quality — contested and thin

Two flags: spring-2026 third-party evals favoring Glean drew **methodological skepticism in press** — vendor-adjacent benchmarks deserve independent replication. And the founder list itself varies by source (third co-founder disputed) — a small thing, but a reminder that even basic facts about private companies come with error bars.
