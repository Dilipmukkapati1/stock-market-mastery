# Part Two — Deep Research II: The Build-Out, Competition, Valuation and Risks

## The technology estate in depth

Alphabet's technical position rests on two assets few companies can replicate: the Gemini model program and the TPU silicon program — the second of which this book treats in proportion (a companion Arc edition covers it exhaustively).

**Gemini** progressed from catch-up to cadence-setter: Gemini 3 (November 2025) took the benchmark lead (1,501 LMArena Elo; GPQA Diamond 91.9%, 93.8% Deep Think); 3.5 Flash headlined I/O in May 2026; 3.6 Flash and 3.5 Flash-Lite followed in July 2026; Gemini 4 is in pre-training with new variants (Argon/Carbon) discussed on the Q3 call. Training and serving run on Google's own fleet via JAX and ML Pathways, and in October 2026 Google and Meta open-sourced **TorchTPU**, narrowing the PyTorch/CUDA ecosystem gap that has been NVIDIA's deepest moat.

**The TPU program**, briefly and in proportion: a decade of generations from v1 (2015) to Ironwood (v7: 4,614 FP8 TFLOPS, 192GB HBM3e, 9,216-chip pods at 42.5 exaflops), with TPU 8t/8i announced in April 2026. Broadcom has co-developed the line since its inception and is contractually locked through 2031 (its April 6, 2026 8-K); MediaTek is reported — not Google-confirmed — on cost-optimized variants. The 2025–26 strategic shift is externalization: Anthropic's commitments (up to 1M TPUs; ~3.5 GW from 2027 via Broadcom; up to $40B of Google investment, as reported), the first TPU *system-sale* revenue recognized in Q2 2026 with the bulk expected through 2027, third-party capacity structures (the Fluidstack/TeraWulf lease backstop; the Blackstone Crux AI venture, May 2026), and Oracle offering TPUs on OCI from 2027 — announced October 27, 2026, the first hyperscaler outside Google to do so. Alphabet still runs NVIDIA at scale (GCP offers B200, GB200, GB300 systems, with Vera Rubin planned), and OpenAI runs large ChatGPT inference on GCP's NVIDIA instances. The TPU is best understood as a cost-and-supply hedge that has grown into a business — not yet a disclosed one: Alphabet does not break out TPU revenue.

## The supply chain of the build-out

Q3's capex split, as disclosed: roughly **65% servers and networking** (depreciated over ~6 years) and **35% data centers** (7–40-year lives) — so the majority of the $195–205B annual program returns to the income statement within six years, and depreciation already rose ~49% year over year in Q3 (to $8.37B in the quarter). The TPU chain runs Google design → Broadcom implementation → TSMC fabrication and CoWoS packaging → HBM from a concentrated supplier set (Samsung and SK hynix the reported principals for TPU HBM3e, per trade-press reporting — labeled, not Alphabet-disclosed). Power is contracted like a utility: the October 6, 2026 Constellation portfolio (3,590 MW, including an 890 MW nuclear uprate PPA on 20-year terms), the Kairos small-modular-reactor program (up to 500 MW; first power targeted 2030), and multi-site developments. And the obligations are already signed: the Q3 10-Q discloses **$924.4 billion of purchase commitments, $130.5 billion of uncommenced leases, and ~$98.5 billion of contingent and backstop arrangements**. Management has told investors to expect third-party capacity to pressure Cloud margins from Q4 — the explicit price of speed. The supply-chain conclusion: Alphabet's build-out is pre-committed at a scale that cannot be unwound if AI demand disappoints; that irreversibility, not any single supplier, is the risk.

## Competition

**In AI infrastructure**, the rivals are Microsoft (Azure at 19% cloud share, with the OpenAI relationship now restructured and its own Maia silicon), Amazon (26% share; Trainium; the industry's largest capex), and NVIDIA — simultaneously supplier, competitor (in systems and cloud via DGX Cloud), and the benchmark every TPU claim is measured against. **In models**, OpenAI and Anthropic compete for the same enterprise budgets while renting Alphabet's fleet — the frenemy structure of Part One. **In advertising**, Meta's net-revenue overtake (EMARKETER forecast) and Amazon's retail-media rise compress the structure from two directions. **In the courtroom**, the competitive position is being edited: the search remedies (September 2025 — no Chrome divestiture, exclusivity banned, data sharing ordered; both sides appealing, DOJ/states cross-appealed April 2026) and the ad-tech remedies (September 2026 — no AdX sale, behavioral terms plus a six-year monitor; Google appealing liability). Neither judgment dismantled the model; both tax its edges indefinitely.

## Valuation

Alphabet's multiples require disassembly. The **trailing P/E (~14.6 at October 30)** divides price by earnings inflated by the H1 SpaceX mark-ups (nine-month EPS $16.33, mostly paper) — it is an artifact, not a valuation. The **forward P/E (~24× at the October 27 close of $320.58; ~21× at October 30's $279.84, book arithmetic)** is the working multiple: a premium to the market that embeds both Cloud's growth and the assumption that capex converts to returns. The stock's 2026 path — a 52-week range of $255–$409, −10.6% year-to-date against the S&P 500's +10.7% — is the market steadily raising the proof standard for that assumption. Analyst opinion after Q3 clusters in two camps visible in the targets (average ~$408 across 58 analysts, as compiled October 30): those underwriting the backlog (Buys to $450) and those underwriting the depreciation wave and dilution (cuts to $300–385). Both camps are pricing the same fact — $200B a year of capex — with different beliefs about its return. That belief, not next quarter's EPS, is the valuation.

## Risks and the two cases

**The bear case** (DA Davidson's $300 target is its current flag): capex converts at mediocre returns; depreciation compounds faster than revenue; Search monetization degrades as AI answers replace the results page (Search already decelerated to +12%); the equity-marks era ends with the stakes (SpaceX above all) marked down as fast as they were marked up; dilution becomes permanent; remedies and the EU stack keep taxing the model. **The bull case** (the $450 targets): Cloud's backlog converts at 36%+ margins, making today's capex look small; the TPU becomes a third-party business with real revenue through 2027; AI surfaces monetize at parity while Gemini's distribution compounds; Waymo is a $126B asset carried at option value; and a ~21× forward multiple for the stack's owner is cheap if any two of those land. **What would change the picture**, in checkable form: Q4's promised 2027 capex guidance (the number and its framing); Cloud margin trajectory against the flagged third-party drag; Search growth vs the +12% line; the first disclosure that lets TPU revenue be sized; and the buyback's return — the single clearest signal that management thinks the conversion is complete.
