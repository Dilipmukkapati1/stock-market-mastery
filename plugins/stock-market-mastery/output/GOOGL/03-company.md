# Part One — Financials

Alphabet's 2026 financials tell two stories at once: an operating business compounding at 25–30%, and a balance sheet being deliberately converted into data centers. Both stories are in the statements; the skill is holding them together.

**The margin stack.** Q3 2026: revenue $127.577B → operating income $40.576B (**31.8% operating margin**, +30% YoY). Services runs at ≈40.7% segment margin; Cloud has climbed to 36.2% (from 23.7% a year earlier — operating leverage as AI capacity fills). Below the operating line sits the year's great distortion: other income (expense) of −$8.221B, arriving at net income of $26.468B (EPS $2.12).

**Quality of earnings: the marks.** Alphabet carries large equity stakes marked to market through the income statement. In Q2 2026 that line added **+$99.0 billion** of unrealized gains (EPS $9.11, of which the operating business earned ≈$2.85). In Q3 it swung to a **−$8.664 billion loss**, mainly the unrealized mark on SpaceX after its June 2026 listing (the release's footnote: −$6.7B of net income, −$0.55 of EPS; underlying Q3 EPS ≈ **$2.67**). Neither number describes operations. The nine-month EPS of $16.33 is mostly paper, the trailing P/E (~14.6 at October 30) is unusable, and any honest valuation starts from the operating lines and the forward multiple (~24× at the October 27 close; ~21× after the post-earnings fall, book arithmetic).

**Cash flow: the real 2026 story.** Operating cash flow was **$42.290B** in Q3 — achieved despite paying the $5.2 billion European Commission fine in the quarter. Capital expenditure was **$51.355B**. Free cash flow: **−$9.065B**, the second consecutive negative quarter; the trailing twelve months remain positive at +$19.747B, down from FY2025's +$73.27B (10-K). The negative FCF is a capex decision, not an operating failure — Q3 capex ran against a full-year guide of $195–205B, and the cash conversion question (does the spend return as Cloud revenue?) is answered so far by the $561B backlog and Cloud's 103% growth. It is the right question to keep asking, quarterly, in exactly this form.

**Balance sheet and capital returns.** At September 30, 2026: cash and marketable securities **$265.098B**; long-term debt **$125.102B** (roughly doubled in a year); total assets above $1 trillion. Dividends continued ($0.22 per share declared for December). But the capital-return regime inverted: buybacks were **$0 in Q3 and $0 year-to-date** (vs $11.5B in Q3 2025), replaced by issuance — $12.4B of stock via the at-the-market program in Q3 and $28.7B of notes. Diluted shares rose 1.4% year over year to 12,368M. The fortress is intact; its direction of travel has reversed, and per-share economics now depend on the build-out's returns rather than on a shrinking share count. The 10-Q adds the obligation stack the balance sheet alone understates: $924.4B of purchase commitments, $130.5B of uncommenced leases, and ~$98.5B of contingent and backstop arrangements (Part Two).

> **Source:** Alphabet Q3 2026 earnings release · 2026-10-28 (income statement, cash flow, balance sheet, dividend); Q2 2026 earnings release · 2026-07-22 (equity-gain quarter); Form 10-Q Q3 2026 · filed 2026-10-29 (commitments); Form 10-K FY2025 · filed 2026-02-05 (FY2025 FCF).
