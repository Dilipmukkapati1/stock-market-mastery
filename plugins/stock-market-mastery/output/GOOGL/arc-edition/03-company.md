# Part One · 3 — Financials

## Q3 2026: Cloud doubles, and the mirage swings the other way

Reported October 28, 2026 (quarter ended September 30, 2026). This is now the latest quarter; the Q2 2026 section below is kept as history.

| Line | Q3 2026 | Year-ago | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $127.58B | $102.35B | +25% |
| Google Services revenue | $96.30B | $87.05B | +11% |
| — Search & other | $63.35B | $56.57B | +12% |
| — YouTube ads | $11.21B | $10.26B | +9% |
| — Subscriptions, platforms & devices | $14.29B | $12.87B | +11% |
| Google Cloud revenue | $30.71B | $15.16B | **+103%** |
| Other Bets revenue | $0.37B | $0.34B | — |
| Operating income | $40.58B (31.8% margin) | $31.23B | +30% |

| **Free cash flow** | **−$9.07B** | +$24.46B | second consecutive negative quarter (trailing-12-month FCF still +$19.7B) |
| Buybacks | **$0** | — | third consecutive quarter at zero (per FactSet via Barron's) |

**Cloud answered the book's central watch item — emphatically.** Growth *accelerated* from Q2's +82% to +103%, against the pre-print worry of deceleration, and the margin expanded to 36.2% despite management's warning that third-party capacity bridging would pressure it. Alphabet credited enterprise AI infrastructure, AI solutions and core GCP services — and said **sales of TPU systems were a meaningful contributor to Cloud revenue growth**, the clearest confirmation yet that the external TPU business flagged throughout Part Two is now moving reported numbers (management guided TPU system sales to keep scaling in Q4, with a meaningful revenue ramp in 2027). Cloud backlog rose to **$561 billion**, with just over 55% expected to convert to revenue within 24 months. Search, by contrast, slowed to +12% (from +17% in Q2) as the tougher year-ago comparisons management had flagged began to bite — total revenue of $127.58 billion landed inside the $127.4–127.9 billion consensus range cited in the front matter, at its low end.

**The mirage, reversed.** Q2's headline profit was inflated by a $99.0 billion unrealized gain; Q3's was depressed by the same line swinging the other way: an **$8.66 billion net loss on equity securities** (versus a $10.73 billion gain a year earlier). Alphabet disclosed the loss reduced net income by $6.7 billion and diluted EPS by **$0.55**. Strip it out — the same adjustment this book applied to Q2 — and underlying EPS was roughly **$2.67**, not $2.12. That is still below the $2.94–3.04 ex-gains consensus the front matter cited, so the earnings miss was real even after the adjustment; it was just smaller than the headline suggested. Operating income, which excludes the line, rose 30% — the operations and the bottom line told opposite stories again. Operating cash flow was also weighed down by a $5.2 billion cash payment closing out the 2018 European Commission fine appeal, and by an inventory build for TPU system sales.

**Capex and the guide.** Capex of $51.36 billion ran at the ~$50-billion-plus quarterly pace the second-half guide implied. Alphabet **maintained the full-year 2026 capex guide at $195–205 billion** — no further raise after July's — and repeated that 2027 capex will "increase significantly," with the actual figure promised on the Q4 call. There is still no formal 2027 number. The company also disclosed $924 billion of future purchase commitments, a reminder that the spending trajectory is substantially contracted already.

**The market's verdict that night:** the shares closed the regular session at $321.89 (+1.6%) and fell roughly 8–9% after hours — the debate, as with Q2, was not demand but the price of supplying it: a second negative-FCF quarter, an ex-gains EPS miss, and Search decelerating while capex climbs.

| Employees | 198,933 | 187,103 | +6% |

**The mirage.** Headline net income was $112.1 billion and EPS $9.11 — up ~294%. Almost all of that is one line: a **$99.0 billion unrealized gain on equity securities** (Alphabet disclosed it added $77.1 billion to net income and $6.26 to EPS; the marks are mostly its private stakes, with coverage attributing the bulk to Anthropic and SpaceX). Strip it out and underlying EPS was roughly **$2.85** — a fine quarter, not a supernatural one. Anyone screening on Alphabet's trailing P/E (~16×) is pricing paper gains; the forward P/E (~24×) is the honest multiple.

**The balance-sheet turn.** In a single quarter Alphabet raised **$49.6 billion of equity** (Class A/C stock plus a mandatory convertible preferred, explicitly to fund AI infrastructure) alongside $20.3 billion of new senior notes, with a further $40 billion at-the-market program authorized and undrawn at quarter-end. Cash and marketable securities stood at $242.5 billion; long-term debt had doubled in six months to $98.2 billion. Like NVIDIA itself in 2026, the AI build-out has turned even the richest balance sheet in tech into an active borrower and issuer. The buyback's disappearance — zero for two quarters after $13.2 billion in the year-ago quarter — is the clearest single symbol of the regime change.

**The demand evidence.** Cloud backlog of **$514 billion**, with management expecting just over half to convert to revenue within 24 months, is the bull case in one number: the spending is chasing contracted demand, led by AI capacity that customers (Anthropic above all) have committed to in gigawatts.

## Context and valuation

- **FY2025:** revenue $402.8B (+15%), operating income $129.0B, net income $132.2B; Cloud $58.7B of revenue with $13.9B of operating income; capex roughly $91B.
- **Capex trajectory:** the 2026 guide has climbed $175–185B (February) → $180–190B (April) → **$195–205B (July)** — and was **held at $195–205B in the Q3 print (October)**; 2027 is still guided only as "increase significantly," with the figure promised on the Q4 call.
- **Stock (October 27, 2026 close):** GOOGL $320.58; market cap ~$3.92 trillion; trailing P/E ~16× (distorted by the equity gains), forward P/E ~24×; 52-week range $255.32–$408.61. The shares fell ~7% the day after the record Q2 print, purely on the capex/FCF story — the market's debate about Alphabet in 2026 is not demand; it is the price of supplying it. Analyst consensus into Q3 sat well above the price (mean target ~$429 across 54 targets in one late-October compilation) — a gap that will either close on capex discipline or on continued Cloud delivery.
