# Part Two — Competition: The Field Around Google

## The accelerator field, as Google faces it

**NVIDIA (~80% of data-center AI accelerator revenue, estimated)** is both competitor and supplier. Its position against Google is paradoxical and strong: Google's cloud is one of its storefronts (GB200/GB300 in production, Vera Rubin next), while Google's TPU is the template every hyperscaler's anti-NVIDIA program copies. NVIDIA's public response to the TPU's external turn — congratulating Google while asserting a generational lead — acknowledges the threat's shape: erosion of the *marginal* gigawatt, not the installed base. Street estimates of share erosion to ~65–70% by 2028 assign the lost points mainly to custom silicon.

**Broadcom — partner, not competitor, but the arc's fulcrum.** Broadcom designs Google's TPUs, is locked in through 2031, and its Google+Anthropic AI revenue is estimated (Mizuho) to roughly double from ~$21 billion (2026) to ~$42 billion (2027). From NVIDIA's perspective Broadcom is the enabler of the entire custom-silicon insurrection; from Google's, it is an indispensable dependency. Arc book 5 reads this company on its own.

**The other hyperscaler programs** — AWS Trainium/Inferentia (Anthropic's *other* silicon home, via Project Rainier), Microsoft's Maia, Meta's MTIA — compete with the TPU for the same prize: internal workloads and, increasingly, third-party demand. Google's leads are time (a decade of production), a flagship in-house model family, and the first external gigawatt-scale customer. Its laggard is openness: Trainium sits inside the largest cloud; the TPU only began leaving home in 2025–26.

**The merchant challengers.** AMD's Instinct line is the conventional second source — and, per Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon on the Meta-TPU reports, the TPU's external push is "incrementally negative" *more for AMD than for NVIDIA*, since it undercuts the second-source pitch itself. A ring of neoclouds (CoreWeave, Crusoe, Fluidstack, and now Google's own Crux AI with Blackstone) competes to operate capacity — some of it TPU-based, most of it NVIDIA-based.

## Named views on the TPU threat (for calibration, not endorsement)

- **Gil Luria, DA Davidson** (late 2025): the most bullish named voice — if Google sells TPUs seriously, it could take ~20% of the AI chip market; he framed a TPU-plus-DeepMind value around ~$900 billion.
- **Brian Nowak, Morgan Stanley**: a "budding TPU sales strategy"; sensitivity of ~+$13 billion 2027 Cloud revenue per ~500k external TPU units (estimate), with TPU procurement modeled at ~5M units in 2027 rising to ~7M in 2028.
- **SemiAnalysis**: the swing technical voice — its 2026 work gave Ironwood cost-per-token edges on specific workloads while documenting NVIDIA's FP4 and latency leads on others; it projects Anthropic passing DeepMind as the largest TPU user by 2029.
- **Mizuho**: the arms-dealer lens on Broadcom (above).
- **Stacy Rasgon, Bernstein**: the skeptic's calibration — TPU externalization as incrementally negative headlines, with the deeper cut landing on AMD.

The spread between Luria and Rasgon is the reader's true uncertainty band. Google's own disclosures — TPU revenue unbroken-out, system sales guided as mostly a 2027 event — say the business is real but young. The next two data points that narrow the band: Alphabet's Q3 2026 print, and any first TPU revenue disclosure in 2027 reporting. The first arrived on October 28, 2026: Alphabet called TPU system sales a meaningful contributor to Cloud's +103% growth quarter and guided the meaningful revenue ramp to 2027 — confirming "real but young," without yet breaking out a TPU revenue figure.
