# Part One · 1 — Management

## The leadership, and the job each of them actually has

**Sundar Pichai, CEO of Alphabet and Google**, runs the whole machine and, since 2023, has been fusing its two halves — the advertising cash engine and the research lab — into a single AI company. His public doctrine on silicon, repeated on the Q2 2026 call, defines this book's subject: *"We offer the industry's broadest range of accelerators from Google and NVIDIA, including the new NVIDIA Vera Rubin platform and TPU 8t and 8i."* Pichai's bet is that Alphabet wins not by choosing between its own chips and NVIDIA's, but by being the place where both are sold — while its own models run overwhelmingly on its own silicon.

**Anat Ashkenazi, CFO**, is the voice of the capex story — and currently the most consequential narrator in the company. She has raised the 2026 capex guide three times in nine months (to $195–205 billion), warned that free cash flow "will remain under pressure," guided 2027 capex to "increase significantly" without a number, and delivered the sentence that marks the TPU's coming of age: Google *"began to recognize revenues from TPU system sales, which we delivered to customer data centers for the first time in Q2."* Read Alphabet's earnings calls for her phrasing; it is unusually precise.

**Thomas Kurian, CEO of Google Cloud**, owns the dual strategy commercially. He fronted the Anthropic TPU announcements and the Blackstone joint venture, and his customer-choice argument — enterprises pick Google Cloud *because* it offers TPUs and NVIDIA side by side — is the sales doctrine behind the numbers in Part Two.

**Amin Vahdat, SVP and Chief Technologist for AI & Infrastructure**, is the public face of the silicon organization: he launched TPU 8t/8i ("two chips for the agentic era," April 2026), fronted the Oracle TPU announcement (October 27, 2026), and said in October 2025 that TPU demand was so oversubscribed that Google was turning customers away, with even seven-to-eight-year-old TPUs running at full utilization. When Google wants the market to understand its infrastructure, Vahdat is the one explaining it.

## The DeepMind reshuffle — the management story of 2026

In August 2026 Alphabet reorganized the lab that builds Gemini. **Demis Hassabis** stepped back from day-to-day leadership to become **Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet** — a new role spanning AGI strategy and safety — while remaining CEO of Isomorphic Labs, the drug-discovery bet that raised over $2 billion in 2026. **Koray Kavukcuoglu**, DeepMind's CTO, became SVP of Google DeepMind reporting directly to Pichai, running Gemini model development, frontier research, and the Gemini app and developer teams. At the same time, **Jeff Dean** (a 27-year Google veteran, co-creator of MapReduce and TensorFlow) and **Sanjay Ghemawat** left to co-found Discovery Loop, an AI-for-science company in which Google is a founding investor and cloud partner. Alphabet's shares fell roughly 5% on the news, and coverage split between two readings: the product-ization of DeepMind under Pichai's direct control, or the beginning of a research brain-drain. For the reader, the significance is structural: the organization that designs Gemini *and co-designs the TPUs it runs on* (Google describes the 8t/8i as designed "in deep collaboration with Google DeepMind") now reports straight to the CEO. (*Leadership change as reported by multiple outlets from Pichai's August 5, 2026 note.*)

## Management quality, in one view

The strengths are continuity, technical depth at the top, and a CFO whose guidance has been candid about costs even when the market punished the candor (the stock fell ~7% on a record quarter in July 2026 because the capex guide rose again). The open questions are equally clear: whether a management team formed in the asset-light advertising era can allocate $200 billion a year of heavy capex with the same discipline; whether the DeepMind reorganization steadies or unsettles the research engine; and whether anyone at Alphabet will ever disclose the TPU business's own numbers — the single most-wanted disclosure this book could not find.
