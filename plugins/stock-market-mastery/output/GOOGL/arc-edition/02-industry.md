# Part One · 2 — The Company

## Three businesses wearing one ticker

Alphabet is a holding structure over three very different operations:

**Google Services** is the cash engine: Search, YouTube, subscriptions, Android/Play, devices. In Q2 2026 it produced $94.5 billion of revenue at a ~41.8% operating margin — Search & other alone was $63.3 billion, growing 17%. This segment funds everything else in this book. Advertising remains ~73% of Alphabet's total revenue (FY2025).

**Google Cloud** is the growth engine and the subject of this arc: $24.8 billion in Q2 2026, up 82% year over year, with operating income of $8.8 billion — a 35.6% margin that a decade of losses made implausible. Cloud now sells three things at once: cloud services (renting Google's TPUs *and* NVIDIA's GPUs), AI products built on Gemini, and — new in 2026 — **TPU systems themselves**, delivered into customers' own data centers. The segment's contracted backlog reached $514 billion at the end of Q2 2026, more than double the level two quarters earlier.

**Other Bets** is the option portfolio: revenue of $382 million against a $1.8 billion operating loss in Q2 2026, dominated by **Waymo**. After its February 2026 raise of $16 billion at a **$126 billion post-money valuation** (Alphabet supplying roughly $13 billion of it, as reported), Waymo is no longer a science project in valuation terms — it is one of the largest "startups" in the world, running over 500,000 paid driverless rides a week (April 2026) across roughly 14–15 U.S. metros, targeting one million rides a week by the end of 2026. Wing (drone delivery, over a million deliveries completed) and Isomorphic Labs (AI drug design) round out the bets with real operating traction.

## The strategic idea

Alphabet's coherence comes from one architectural choice it made a decade before its rivals: **build the whole AI stack itself** — the models (DeepMind), the chips (TPU), the interconnect and data-center fabrics, the software stack (JAX/XLA and now TorchTPU), and the products (Search, YouTube, Cloud) that monetize them. Vertical integration is why Google's AI economics differ from every other hyperscaler's: when Gemini answers a query, no merchant silicon margin leaks out of the system. It is also why Alphabet is simultaneously NVIDIA's most credible long-term rival and one of its largest distribution channels — a paradox Part Two takes apart in "The NVIDIA Question."

FY2025 gives the scale in one line: revenue of $402.8 billion (its first year above $400 billion), operating income of $129.0 billion, and a Cloud segment that earned $13.9 billion on $58.7 billion of revenue. The company that emerges in 2026 is bigger, more profitable at the operating line — and, for the first time in its history, a ravenous *consumer* of capital rather than a pure generator of it. That transformation, driven by silicon, is the story of the financials section and of Part Two.
