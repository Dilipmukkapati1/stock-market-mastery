# Part Two — Deep Research

## 1 · A Decade of TPUs: From Internal Experiment to Industry Force

The TPU exists because of a 2013 internal estimate: if Google's users spoke to search for just minutes a day, neural-network inference could require roughly *doubling* Google's data-center compute. Buying that much conventional hardware was unthinkable, so Google built its own. Every generation since has followed the same logic — design the chip around Google's actual workloads, co-designed with the models, the network, and the compiler.

| Generation | Year | Designed for | Per-chip peak | Memory | Largest pod |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 2015 | Inference only | 92 TOPS (INT8) | 8 GB DDR3 | Single board (PCIe) |
| v2 | 2017 | Training + inference | 45 TFLOPS (bf16) | 16 GB HBM | 256 chips |
| v3 | 2018 | Training at scale | 123 TFLOPS | 32 GB | 1,024 chips |
| v4 | 2021 | Training flagship | 275 TFLOPS | 32 GB | 4,096 chips; 3D torus, optical switching |
| v5e | 2023 | Cost-optimized | 197 TFLOPS | 16 GB | 256 chips |
| v5p | 2023 | Training flagship | 459 TFLOPS | 95 GB | 8,960 chips |
| v6e "Trillium" | 2024 | Balanced | 918 TFLOPS | 32 GB | 256 chips (multislice beyond 100k) |
| **v7 "Ironwood"** | Announced Apr 2025; GA Mar 31, 2026 | **Inference-first** (also training/RL) | **4,614 TFLOPS (FP8)** | **192 GB HBM3e @ 7.37 TB/s** | **9,216 chips = 42.5 exaflops** |
| **TPU 8t** | Announced Apr 22, 2026 | Large-scale pre-training | **12.6 PFLOPS (FP4)** | 216 GB | 9,600 chips = **121 exaflops** |
| **TPU 8i** | Announced Apr 22, 2026 | Post-training, serving, reasoning | **10.1 PFLOPS (FP4)** | 288 GB, 384 MB SRAM | Up to 1,152 chips (Boardfly) |

*Specs as published by Google (Cloud documentation and blogs); early-generation details compiled from Google documentation.*

Three observations matter more than the rows. **First, the inference pivot:** Ironwood is the first TPU designed explicitly for inference — the "thinking" era — because inference, not training, is where the durable volume (and the operating cost of the AI economy) now lives. **Second, the 2026 split:** the eighth generation divides into a training chip (8t, with the new Virgo fabric linking over 134,000 chips) and an inference chip (8i, with three times Ironwood's on-chip SRAM for KV cache and a latency-optimized Boardfly topology) — Google now optimizes silicon per phase of the AI workload, something a merchant vendor selling one flagship finds harder to do. **Third, the availability asterisk:** as of late October 2026, Google Cloud's own documentation still listed TPU 7x as the latest *available* generation, with the 8 family "coming soon" after a "later in 2026" GA guide. Announced silicon is not shipped silicon — a discipline this arc will apply to every company's roadmap, including NVIDIA's.

**Who actually builds them.** Google leads architecture and co-designs with DeepMind; **Broadcom** — the design partner since the program's inception — translates specifications into silicon (its SerDes interfaces, ASIC implementation, and TSMC fabrication/packaging coordination). The recurring fear that Google would internalize or replace Broadcom was put to rest on **April 6, 2026**, when Broadcom's Form 8-K disclosed a long-term agreement to develop and supply future TPU generations plus rack networking **through 2031**. Alongside it, **MediaTek** is *reported* (not confirmed by Google) to handle cost-optimized variants — including the inference 8i, codenamed "Zebrafish" in trade press — at a reported 20–30% lower cost, with Broadcom on the training 8t ("Sunfish"). Mizuho estimates Broadcom's AI revenue from Google plus Anthropic at ~$21 billion in 2026, doubling to ~$42 billion in 2027 — the arms-dealer economics this arc revisits in the Broadcom book.

## 2 · The Economics Fight: Is the TPU Actually Cheaper?

This is the technical heart of the NVIDIA-risk question, and the honest answer as of October 2026 is: **it depends on the workload, the precision, and whose configurations you believe — and the dispute is live among experts using the same data.**

**Google's claims** (vendor claims, labeled as such): Ironwood delivers over 4× Trillium's per-chip performance at 2× the performance per watt; TPU 8t up to **2.7× better performance-per-dollar than Ironwood** for large-scale training; TPU 8i up to **+80% perf/$** for low-latency inference; both 8-series chips at roughly 2× Ironwood's perf/W.

**Third-party estimates point TPU's way — with caveats.** SemiAnalysis (November 2025) estimated TPU infrastructure total cost of ownership ~**30% below NVIDIA GB200** for a buyer, and ~**44% below from Google's internal perspective** (no merchant margin on its own silicon). SemiAnalysis's InferenceX work (September 7, 2026) modeled FP8 serving of Qwen3.5-397B at **$0.181 per million tokens for Ironwood vs $0.222 (B200) and $0.276 (B300)** — up to ~50% better performance per dollar at some operating points. But the same study showed Ironwood's time-to-first-token at high concurrency *worse* (5.41s vs 3.75s/2.40s), per-chip throughput only ~5% ahead of B200, and no native FP4 — the precision where NVIDIA's newest parts shine.

**Then the fight.** In October 2026, Google submitted its own TPU v7 results to SemiAnalysis's agentic-inference benchmark claiming **1.42–1.67× better performance-per-dollar than GB300 NVL72**. A detailed counter-reading argued that, best-configuration against best-configuration on SemiAnalysis's own dashboard, GB300 delivers a multiple of the TPU's tokens per dollar — hinging on choices (FP8 vs NVFP4, aggregated vs disaggregated serving, chip counts) that swing the answer by an order of magnitude. Both cannot be casually true, and neither is a lab-certified constant.

**The book's verdict:** for *Google's own* workloads — models designed for the chip, compiled through its own stack, at internal silicon cost — the TPU's cost advantage is real and structural: (a) no ~75%-gross-margin merchant layer, (b) chip, interconnect (ICI/optical switching/Jupiter/Virgo), compiler and model co-designed as one system, (c) inference priced from internal cost. For *arbitrary external* workloads, the advantage narrows to workload-dependent estimates — and the 2026 evidence (Anthropic's gigawatts, Oracle's adoption) suggests large, sophisticated buyers with suitable models are finding the economics compelling at scale. What the data does *not* support is a single universal "TPUs are X% cheaper" number — and any source offering one should be read as marketing, whichever logo it carries.

**The software variable.** The TPU's historical tax was its stack: JAX-first, with PyTorch bolted on awkwardly. **TorchTPU** (open-sourced October 20, 2026, built with Meta's PyTorch team) is Google's attempt to delete that tax — native PyTorch on TPU, 98% of popular Hugging Face models running unmodified in reported testing, ~94% of JAX throughput in a published training test, and reported production use at Meta. If TorchTPU matures, the addressable market for TPU capacity stops being "teams willing to rewrite in JAX" and becomes "everyone" — the precondition for the external business in the next chapter.

## 3 · The Year the TPU Left the Building

For a decade the TPU was a weapon Google kept at home: rentable on Google Cloud, never sold, never placed elsewhere. Between October 2025 and October 2026 that wall came down, in a sequence worth memorizing because the rest of this arc keeps referencing it:

1. **October 23, 2025 — Anthropic.** Expansion to *up to one million TPUs*, tens of billions of dollars, well over a gigawatt online in 2026. Kurian's quote — Anthropic chose TPUs for "the strong price-performance and efficiency its teams have seen" — was the first public proof that a frontier lab with every option (Trainium, NVIDIA) was betting its scale on Google silicon.
2. **April 6, 2026 — the tri-party lock-in.** Anthropic, Google and Broadcom agreed **multiple gigawatts of next-generation TPU capacity from 2027** (~3.5 GW via Broadcom per its 8-K); the same day, Broadcom's agreement to develop and supply Google's future TPUs **through 2031** became public. The designer, the builder, and the biggest customer were contractually aligned for five years.
3. **April 24, 2026 — the investment.** Google committed up to **$40 billion** into Anthropic ($10 billion immediately at a $350 billion valuation; $30 billion contingent), alongside at least 5 GW of compute over five years (Reuters). Supplier financing the customer's growth — and booking the equity gains (see the earnings mirage in chapter 5).
4. **Q2 2026 — systems, sold.** Alphabet confirmed it had **delivered TPU systems to customer data centers and recognized the first revenue**, with the vast majority of those agreements' revenue expected in 2027. Alphabet even amended its segment description: Cloud now "generates product revenues primarily from the sale of TPU systems." Meanwhile Google was already backstopping ~$3.2 billion of Fluidstack's lease at TeraWulf's Lake Mariner campus (SEC filing, August 2025) to place TPUs with third-party operators.
5. **May 18, 2026 — Blackstone.** A joint venture ($5 billion initial Blackstone equity; first 500 MW online 2027; launched as **Crux AI** in September 2026, led by Google's infrastructure veteran Benjamin Treynor Sloss) to sell TPU capacity as a service *outside* Google Cloud.
6. **October 27, 2026 — Oracle.** TPUs on **Oracle Cloud Infrastructure from 2027** — "the first time Google TPUs will be offered on a hyperscale cloud platform outside Google" — with Oracle describing a multi-year, multi-gigawatt commitment.

Around this spine orbit the *reported* items: Meta's TPU talks and a reported multibillion-dollar rental signing (The Information, November 2025/February 2026 — **unconfirmed by either company**); October 2026 reporting on Anthropic's IPO filings showing $417 billion of compute commitments, $125.2 billion of TPU leases, and a ~$60–63 billion Broadcom-organized financing ("Project North Star") — plausible, consistent with the disclosed trajectory, but **not yet primary-verified**, and flagged here accordingly.

**Sizing it (estimates only):** analyst models in October 2026 reporting put external TPU revenue at roughly **$8 billion in 2026 rising to $51–75 billion+ in 2027**; Morgan Stanley sensitivities imply every ~500k external TPU units ≈ +$13 billion of 2027 Cloud revenue. Alphabet discloses none of it directly — TPU money hides inside Cloud's $24.8 billion quarter and $514 billion backlog. Even the low end of those estimates describes a business that, three years ago, did not exist.

## 4 · The NVIDIA Question

*This chapter appears in every book of the Custom Silicon Arc, each time answered from that company's side of the table.*

**Google's position is unique among the hyperscalers: it is simultaneously NVIDIA's most credible silicon rival, one of its largest sales channels, and — for its own first-party workloads — barely its customer at all.**

Start with what Google *buys*. Google Cloud's NVIDIA catalog is current and complete: A3 (H100/H200), A4 (B200), A4X (GB200 NVL72, generally available May 2025), **A4X Max (GB300 NVL72), shipping in production as of October 2026**, and **A5X (Vera Rubin NVL72), with general availability planned for November 2026** — NVIDIA said in August 2026 that Rubin racks were already running at Google Cloud. These instances are not a token offering: **OpenAI runs large-scale inference, including for ChatGPT, on Google Cloud's A4X/A4X Max fleet.** Google's doctrine, in Pichai's words, is "the industry's broadest range of accelerators from Google and NVIDIA" — because cloud customers with CUDA estates demand NVIDIA, and refusing to sell it would simply hand those customers to Azure and AWS.

Now what Google *doesn't* buy. Search, YouTube, AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini's training and serving run predominantly on TPUs — Google's largest internal AI workloads were never NVIDIA's to lose in this product cycle. The substitution risk to NVIDIA from Google is therefore concentrated in three channels:

1. **First-party growth that never touches NVIDIA.** Every new Gemini-class training run or inference fleet Google adds is TPU by default. NVIDIA's exposure isn't a lost account; it's an absent one — the world's second-largest AI compute buyer simply isn't in its funnel for internal demand.
2. **Third-party demand routed to TPU.** Anthropic's million-TPU commitment, the system sales, Crux AI, and TPUs on Oracle are all workloads that, absent a credible TPU, would have been contested between NVIDIA and Trainium. This is the *new* channel of risk — it converts Google from a neutral landlord into a competing silicon vendor — and it is the one that moved markets: the November 2025 report of Meta's TPU talks knocked roughly $200 billion from NVIDIA's market value in a day (contemporaneous reporting), and NVIDIA took the unusual step of publicly asserting it remained "a generation ahead" while congratulating Google.
3. **Cloud shelf space.** Every enterprise that TorchTPU makes comfortable on TPU instances is GPU rental revenue that doesn't occur — a slow, compounding channel, gated by software maturity.

**The numbers that can and cannot be said.** No credible source discloses Google's purchases from NVIDIA or a Google-specific share of NVIDIA's data-center revenue; NVIDIA does not name customers and Alphabet does not break out accelerator spending (its capex is ~60% "servers" in aggregate). At the group level, NVIDIA's CFO has put hyperscalers at roughly **half** of data-center revenue. The honest sentence for this book: *Google's direct share of NVIDIA's revenue is undisclosed, plausibly high-single-digits to low-teens percent (inference from group shares — an estimate, not a reported figure), and structurally shrinking as a share of Google's own compute even while absolute GPU purchases for Cloud resale keep growing.*

**Resolve her paradox here — the reader's original question:** how can the CSPs' share of NVIDIA's revenue fall while NVIDIA's revenue climbs? Google's numbers show the mechanism. Alphabet's 2026 capex guide is $195–205 billion — roughly double FY2025's ~$91 billion. Within a wallet growing that fast, NVIDIA can lose *share of Google's compute* every quarter (TPU absorbs first-party and routed third-party demand) while its *absolute* Google-channel revenue still grows (Cloud customers' Blackwell and Rubin rentals scale with an 82%-growth segment). Percentages describe the mix; dollars describe the business. The risk to NVIDIA is not that the CSPs stop buying — it is that the *marginal* gigawatt increasingly goes to silicon NVIDIA doesn't sell, at exactly the moment the CSPs' systems start competing for *other people's* gigawatts too. Street estimates holding NVIDIA at ~80% of data-center accelerator revenue today, eroding to ~65–70% by 2028 mainly to custom silicon, are this same logic priced in — erosion, not collapse.

**Verdict for Google:** the TPU is a real, compounding competitive fact — the only hyperscaler silicon program with a decade of production history, a frontier model family running on it exclusively, and now external customers at gigawatt scale. It is not, on this evidence, an NVIDIA killer: NVIDIA's Google-channel business is growing, the economics gap is workload-dependent and disputed, and the TPU's external ramp is a 2027 revenue story, not a 2026 one. Watch: TPU 8 availability and pricing, the first disclosed TPU revenue figures (if ever), Anthropic's Anthropic-scale follow-through, and whether TorchTPU converts curiosity into migrations.

## 5 · The Capex Supercycle and the Earnings Mirage

Alphabet's 2026 is a study in financial transformation. Capex guidance: **$175–185 billion in February → $180–190 billion in April → $195–205 billion in July**, with 2027 to "increase significantly" (analyst guesses for 2027 ran from ~$262 billion to ~$330 billion in previews — a spread that itself measures the uncertainty). Q2 capex of $44.9 billion ran at ~2× the year-ago quarter; the guide implies roughly **$60 billion per quarter in the second half**. Free cash flow went negative (−$5.9 billion in Q2, though +$53.3 billion trailing-twelve-months) for the first time in the company's public history; buybacks — roughly $13 billion in the year-ago quarter — were **zero for a second straight quarter**; and the company raised $49.6 billion of equity plus $20.3 billion of notes in a single quarter, with a $40 billion ATM program authorized behind it. Long-term debt doubled in six months to $98.2 billion. Depreciation from the 2025–2027 server cohorts will land on the income statement from 2027 onward, whether or not revenue keeps pace.

Layered over this is the earnings mirage: Q2's reported net income of $112.1 billion contains a **$99.0 billion unrealized gain on equity securities** — marks on private holdings, attributed mostly to Anthropic and SpaceX stakes (Q1 2026 had a similar $37.7 billion). Underlying EPS was ~$2.85, not $9.11; trailing P/E of ~16× is an artifact, and the ~24× forward multiple is the working number. There is also a reflexive loop worth naming: Google invests in Anthropic → Anthropic commits to Google TPUs → Anthropic's valuation rises → Google books equity gains → Google's reported earnings look spectacular while its free cash flow is negative. None of this is hidden — Alphabet discloses the gain's EPS impact explicitly — but a reader who does not strip it will misunderstand both Alphabet's profitability and its valuation.

**The bull case** is that this is the best kind of spending: contracted. Cloud +82% at a 35.6% margin; backlog $514 billion with >50% converting within 24 months; Search throwing off $39.5 billion of operating income in the quarter to fund it; vertical integration lowering Alphabet's cost per token below any rival renting merchant silicon; and the TPU external business as unpriced optionality. Consensus targets (~$429 mean) sit far above the $320 stock. **The bear case** is arithmetic and time: negative and falling FCF, dilution replacing buybacks, a depreciation wave not yet in earnings, third-party capacity bridging already pressuring Cloud margins in Q3, hardware sales (TPU systems) being structurally lower-margin and lumpy — management's own warning — and the possibility that 2027's "significant increase" prices AI capacity at the top of a cycle. The stock's ~7% fall on a record quarter is the market holding both thoughts at once. **The first resolution data has now arrived — Q3 2026, reported October 28:** Cloud accelerated to +103% at a 36.2% margin with backlog up to $561 billion (bull-case evidence), while free cash flow fell further to −$9.07 billion, buybacks stayed at zero for a third straight quarter, the 2026 capex guide was held at $195–205 billion, and 2027 remained "increase significantly" with no number until the Q4 call (bear-case evidence, unresolved). The shares fell roughly 8–9% after hours — the market, again, holding both thoughts at once. Full detail in Part One · 3.

## 6 · Competition and Regulation

**Cloud AI competition.** Synergy Research's Q2 2026 shares — AWS 28%, Azure 20%, **Google Cloud 15% (a record high**, up from 13% a year earlier) on a $143.4 billion market growing 43% — describe Google's position precisely: third, but the fastest-growing of the three, with AWS still adding more absolute dollars per quarter. Its differentiation claim is the full stack — TPU silicon, Gemini models, data platform, security (Wiz) — which is why this book treats the TPU as strategy, not engineering trivia. The direct rivals' silicon programs get their own arc books: Amazon's Trainium (book 2), Microsoft's Maia (book 3). Ironically, Oracle has now joined Google's side of the board by reselling TPUs, while remaining one of NVIDIA's biggest customers — the industry's alliances are becoming deliberately non-exclusive.

**Regulation — the overhang shrank.** Two federal courts, two rejected break-ups. In the **search case** (Judge Mehta), September 2025 remedies banned exclusive default contracts and ordered sharing of search index and user-interaction data with rivals — but no Chrome divestiture and no ban on default payments. Both sides have appealed (the DOJ and states cross-appealed in April 2026 seeking stronger relief), leaving a years-long tail with behavioral remedies in force meanwhile. In the **ad-tech case** (Judge Brinkema), September 2026 remedies again rejected the DOJ's requested divestiture (of AdX) in favor of behavioral remedies and a six-year compliance monitor; Google will appeal the liability findings to the Fourth Circuit. The pattern — behavioral, not structural — is the largest single de-risking of Alphabet equity across 2025–2026. What remains is chronic, not existential: compliance costs, data-sharing that modestly arms rivals, and default economics that must now be re-won in annual bidding.

The live version of this risk is now Q4 2026 and the guided TPU-system-sales ramp into 2027.* (2) TPU 8 slippage: documentation still showing 7x as the shipping flagship deep into 2027. (3) Anthropic concentration: Alphabet's supplier-investor-customer triangle is a strength until Anthropic stumbles; its IPO filings (October 2026, as reported) will be this arc's next primary source. (4) The capex guide for 2027: any number at or above ~$300 billion without matching backlog growth reopens the bear case. (5) Research-organization drift after the DeepMind reshuffle: two consecutive Gemini generations losing the frontier would undermine the entire vertical stack, silicon included.
