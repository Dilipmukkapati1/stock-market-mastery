# Part One — The Company

Alphabet is three businesses sharing one infrastructure: an advertising giant that funds everything, a cloud that has become a second engine of genuine scale, and a portfolio of long-dated options. Q3 2026's segment table is the company in miniature.

| Q3 2026 | Revenue | Operating income | Margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Services | $96.302B (+11%) | $39.157B | ≈40.7% |
| Google Cloud | $30.709B (+103%) | $11.121B | 36.2% |
| Other Bets | $0.372B | −$2.047B | — |
| Alphabet-level activities | — | −$7.655B | — |
| **Total** | **$127.577B (+25%)** | **$40.576B** | **31.8%** |

**Google Services** (75.5% of revenue) is the attention business: Search & other ($63.350B, +12%), YouTube advertising ($11.207B, +9%), the Network ($7.455B, +1%), and subscriptions, platforms and devices ($14.290B, +11% — YouTube Premium and TV, Google One, Play). Its economics are auction economics: advertisers bid for intent, and Alphabet pays about a fifth of advertising revenue back out as traffic acquisition costs ($16.366B in Q3) to the distribution partners — above all Apple — who control the doorways. The business model is a flywheel: free products aggregate billions of users; usage generates the data and distribution that make the ads valuable and the AI better; the profits fund infrastructure that improves the products. Each turn widens the gap a challenger must cross — the moat claim Part Two tests.

**Google Cloud** (24.1% of revenue) is the enterprise business: infrastructure, data, and AI services sold to companies, now including the TPU systems that used to be purely internal. Its transformation is the company's biggest internal story: from $58.7 billion of revenue in FY2025 to a $30.7 billion *quarter* a year later, with operating margin expanding from 23.7% to 36.2% in twelve months. Cloud is no longer a growth story subsidized by ads; it produced $11.1 billion of operating income in the quarter. Its demand visibility is unusual for the industry: a **$561 billion backlog**, over 55% expected to convert within 24 months.

**Other Bets** (0.3% of revenue) is the options portfolio, dominated by Waymo. It loses about $2 billion a quarter — roughly 5% of consolidated operating income, an affordable option premium at Alphabet's margins — against assets like a robotaxi business valued at $126 billion in its February 2026 raise and running more than 500,000 paid rides a week.

**Alphabet-level activities** (−$7.655B) are the unallocated center: much of the shared AI research engine and corporate cost. Read it as the R&D commons whose output both Services and Cloud sell — the structural reason Alphabet can train frontier models and serve billions of consumer queries off one fleet.

The company's stated strategy, consistent across calls and filings, is to be the full-stack AI platform: own the models (Gemini), the silicon (TPU), the infrastructure (Cloud), and the consumer surfaces (Search, YouTube, Android) — and let each layer feed the others. Part Two examines whether the stack is a moat or a capital trap.
